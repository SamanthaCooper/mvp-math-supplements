# Curriculum coherence notes

Working observations about MVP sections where the in-class task and Ready, Set, Go exercises feel disjointed, or where the purpose of a transition is unclear. This is a companion to the cumulative curriculum map: the map records where topics occur; these notes consider how the topics fit together within a lesson.

Keep observations tied to specific packet locations. Distinguish new instruction, extensions, and review, and record useful connections as well as gaps. Mixed practice can be worthwhile; these notes should explain the particular difficulty rather than treating every change of topic as a defect. Entries are provisional and may be expanded, revised, or used for a later analysis. They do not automatically require changes to the student-facing supplements.

## Secondary Math I · Module 3 · Section 3.6

Recorded October 12, 2026, while reviewing the Section 3.6 supplement. Source: May 2016 student packet, printed pp. 27–33 (`MVP 1/m1_mod3_se_52016f.pdf`, stored locally).

### Observation

The section has a broad theme of interpreting functions across graphs, tables, rules, and situations, but its homework is only partly focused on the distinctive multiple-function work in the in-class task. A student can reasonably feel that class and homework are practicing different things.

The in-class task itself includes review of individual functions. Its distinctive work with multiple functions includes comparing outputs, finding where functions agree, adding their outputs, and comparing rates or growth. In particular, Main 9–14 compares two graphs and constructs their sum; Main 15–19 compares two functions in a table; Main 20–25 works with several rules and relations. These are not necessarily all new concepts in 3.6: earlier sections have already introduced related work.

### How the components connect

- **In class · pp. 27–29:** reads and describes individual functions, compares and combines multiple functions, and constructs graphs satisfying stated conditions. It is already a fairly broad practice task.
- **Ready 1–4 · p. 30:** finds intersections algebraically by setting two function expressions equal. This is a useful connection to the in-class questions about where functions agree, and extends graph-based intersection work with an algebraic method.
- **Set 5–8 · p. 31:** reads inputs and outputs from individual graphs. This reinforces function notation and graph reading, but provides little practice comparing or combining functions.
- **Set 9–10 · p. 32:** interprets and evaluates a walking-distance model and an exponential population model. These connect through function notation, but move into contextual applications. The population threshold question adds a separate challenge: finding the first whole-week input for which the model exceeds a target.
- **Go 11–13 · p. 33:** describes one function per graph using domain, range, direction, and maximum/minimum values. These ideas are explained in Section 3.3, and domain/range/direction are also practiced in Section 3.4. Go is primarily review. There are three separate functions to describe; the exercises do not ask students to compare multiple functions on the same graph.

### Why it feels disjointed

The multiple-function work is a noticeable part of class, but much of Set and all of Go return to individual functions. Ready has the clearest connection to comparing functions. Set also shifts from graphical interpretation to contextual models, with little explanation of the transition. The shared label “functions” connects the topics, but is too broad by itself to explain their instructional sequence.

This is mixed review and extension rather than a focused sequence in which one class idea receives sustained homework practice. That distinction matters when deciding how much explanation each part of the supplement needs.

### Implications for the supplement

Explain the worthwhile multiple-function ideas in the in-class material and make the connection to algebraic intersections in Ready clear. Treat individual graph reading and graph-feature descriptions as review, with links to earlier explanations and answer keys where appropriate. Give contextual problems separate worked help when their wording or mathematics warrants it, as with Set 9–10.

Avoid implying that all Ready, Set, Go exercises directly practice the same class technique. No additional redesign or public critique is decided by this note.
