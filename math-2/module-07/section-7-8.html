An angle stays fixed while its arc grows

Concentric circles share a center. The same two rays cut a 40° angle on every circle, even though the curved distance between them grows with radius. Main 1: the corresponding angles on both inner circles are 40°, 50°, 60°, 90°, and 120°, just as on the outside. They sum to 360°.

Main 2–3: calculate once, then scale

Use s = (θ/360)2πr. For the 40° arc at radius 10 feet, s = (40/360)(20π) = 20π/9 ft. At radius 20, both radius and arc double: s = 40π/9 ft. At radius 30, they triple: s = 20π/3 ft.

In each list below, the angle order is 40°, 50°, 60°, 90°, 120°. These are the exact arc-length checks for Main 2:

  • r = 10 ft: 20π/9, 25π/9, 10π/3, 5π, 20π/3 ft.
  • r = 20 ft: 40π/9, 50π/9, 20π/3, 10π, 40π/3 ft.
  • r = 30 ft: 20π/3, 25π/3, 10π, 15π, 20π ft.

Main 3: at a fixed angle, lengths scale like the radii (1:2:3); at a fixed radius, lengths scale like the angles (40:50:60:90:120). For each circle, the five arcs add to 2πr.

Main 4: divide out the circle’s size

Divide arc length by radius:

\[\frac{s}{r}=\frac{(\theta/360)2\pi r}{r}=\frac{\theta\pi}{180}\]

The radius cancels because r > 0. Each of the three rows in Main 4 is therefore identical: 2π/9, 5π/18, π/3, π/2, 2π/3, approximately 0.698, 0.873, 1.047, 1.571, 2.094. These ratios are the angles’ radian measures.

A radian measure tells how many radius-lengths fit along the arc: if s/r = 1, the arc is exactly one radius long and the angle is one radian. Use the same length unit for s and r before dividing. Their units cancel.

Main 5–6: area grows with the square of radius

Use A = (θ/360)πr². For example, the 40° sector out to radius 10 feet has area 100π/9 ft². At radius 20 its area is 400π/9 ft², four times as large. At radius 30 it is nine times as large.

Main 5, page 48: sectors versus ring pieces

The student packet asks for “areas of the sectors for the different rings.” The entries here are full sectors from the center to the stated radius, matching the “Distance from Center” rows. For only the part between two circles, subtract the inner sector from the outer sector.

Main 5 checks, in the same angle order as above:

  • r = 10 ft: 100π/9, 125π/9, 50π/3, 25π, 100π/3 ft².
  • r = 20 ft: 400π/9, 500π/9, 200π/3, 100π, 400π/3 ft².
  • r = 30 ft: 100π, 125π, 150π, 225π, 300π ft².
  • r = 40 ft: 1600π/9, 2000π/9, 800π/3, 400π, 1600π/3 ft².

Main 6: for fixed angle, radii 10:20:30:40 produce areas 1:4:9:16. Dividing sector area by r² gives the same value at every radius: θπ/360. With radian measure x = θπ/180, the formulas become s = rx and A = r²x/2. Use radians in these shorter formulas.

Volume counts inside; surface area counts outside

A rectangular prism has V = lwh. Its three different face areas are lw, lh, wh, each appearing twice, so SA = 2(lw + lh + wh).

A closed cylinder has V = πr²h. Its surface includes two circular ends and an unrolled rectangle of width 2πr and height h, so SA = 2πr² + 2πrh. In 2, the 2-inch segment runs from the circle center to its edge, so it is the radius.

  2. (a) V = 24π in³; (b) SA = 32π in².
  3. (a) V = 1920 in³; (b) SA = 992 in².

Radian measure describes the angle, not the circle size

A half-circle’s arc is πr. Dividing by r gives π radians for any radius. For a 45° sector, the fraction of a turn is 1/8: s = πr/4, x = s/r = π/4, and area = πr²/8.

For 5 at radius 10, s = 5π/2 units, x = π/4 radians, and area = 25π/2 square units. Doubling the radius doubles the arc and quadruples the area, while x stays fixed.

5: each entry is arc length; radians; sector area.

  • r = 10: 5π/2; π/4; 25π/2.
  • r = 20: 5π; π/4; 50π.
  • r = 30: 15π/2; π/4; 225π/2.

Section 7.8 · Go · 8–13 · page 51

Same angle, two radii

Both sectors have angle 135° = 3π/4 radians. The larger radius 6 cm is three times the smaller radius 2 cm. Thus its arc is three times as long and its area nine times as large.

For the small circle, s = 2(3π/4) = 3π/2 cm and A = (1/2)(2²)(3π/4) = 3π/2 cm². The matching numbers have different units and represent different quantities.
