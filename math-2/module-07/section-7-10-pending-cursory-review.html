Section 7.10 · Main · 1–8 · pages 57–60

A scale factor acts on every length

Scaling a figure by k > 0 multiplies every length by k. A rectangle’s area becomes (kl)(kw) = k²lw. A box’s volume becomes (kl)(kw)(kh) = k³lwh. Surface area also has two length factors, so it scales by k².

\[\text{length factor}=k\]\[\text{area factor}=k^2\]\[\text{volume factor}=k^3\]

Main 1–3: explain “twice as big” with examples

Take two footprints of area 2.5 square units: a 1-by-2.5 rectangle and a right triangle with base 5 and height 1. Doubling every length changes them to a 2-by-5 rectangle and a triangle with base 10 and height 2. Each new area is 10 square units, four times its original area.

For 2, give each original footprint a height of 1 unit to make a prism: each volume is 2.5 cubic units. Doubling every dimension, including height, gives base area 10 and height 2, hence volume 20 cubic units. A grid of half-unit cubes can model the rectangle; the triangular solid can be compared by cutting a rectangular prism in half.

3: these examples interpret “twice as big” as a length scale factor of 2. Benji instead doubled footprint area. To double area under similarity, the length factor would be √2. Those are different enlargements.

With the specified length factors 1,2,4 for the three castles, the footprints are 2.5,10,40 ft². Their total is 52.5 ft², which exceeds the 50 ft² limit by 2.5 ft². Benji’s 17.5 ft² total does not apply to these length-scaled castles.

Main 4–6: why the shape does not change the factors

4: divide a polygon into triangles. Scaling multiplies every triangle’s base and perpendicular height by 2, so each area becomes four times as large. Adding them proves the same factor for the entire polygon. Circles agree too: π(2r)² = 4πr². The enlarged area depends on the original area and scale factor, not the outline.

5: the perimeter doubles because each boundary length doubles. 6: if the whole one-inch-thick foam solid is scaled by 2, its thickness becomes two inches while its footprint area quadruples. Volume becomes eight times as large. Keeping thickness at one inch would give four times the volume, but that would not scale every dimension.

Main 7: a square-based prism

The original prism is 2 by 2 by 5 inches. V = 2·2·5 = 20 in³. Its full surface has two 2-by-2 bases and four 2-by-5 sides: SA = 2·4 + 4·10 = 48 in². Each next castle doubles the preceding castle’s lengths, so the largest is four times the smallest in length.

7a–c: volumes 20,160,1280 in³. 7d: base perimeters 8,16,32 in. 7e: total surface areas 48,192,768 in². Check with enlarged dimensions 4×4×10 and 8×8×20.

Main 8: a cylindrical column

The original cylinder has radius 1 in and height 12 in. Its volume is π·1²·12 = 12π in³. Its complete mold surface includes both circular bases: SA = 2πr² + 2πrh = 2π + 24π = 26π in².

8a–c: volumes 12π,96π,768π in³. 8d: base circumferences 2π,4π,8π in. 8e: full surface areas 26π,104π,416π in². The three radii are 1,2,4 in and heights 12,24,48 in.

Section 7.10 · Ready · 1–5 · page 61

Construct a center, then compare the angles

For 1–2, draw two nonparallel chords in each circle and construct each chord’s perpendicular bisector. Their intersection is the center. The packet circles have no coordinate axes, so the answer is a constructed point, not a numerical coordinate.

3: the center is equally far from each chord’s endpoints, since those distances are radii. Therefore it lies on both bisectors. Two nonparallel bisectors meet in one point, pinpointing the center.

4: choose endpoints A,B and an angle vertex C elsewhere on the circle, outside the selected arc AB. Draw OA,OB,CA,CB. Then ∠ACB is half the central angle intercepting that same arc. 5: draw two tangents at A,B meeting outside the circle. Their angle is 180° minus the central angle for minor arc AB, because each radius is perpendicular to its tangent. Use a minor central angle less than 180° so the two tangents meet.

Check your work — 1–5

1–2: center = intersection of two chord perpendicular bisectors. 3: equal endpoint radii place the center on both bisectors; uniqueness fixes the point. 4: central angle = twice corresponding inscribed angle. 5: central angle + corresponding circumscribed angle = 180°. Draw the requested chords, angles and centers on your packet; those marks are not supplied here.

Calculate the first solid, then use the proper power

For a rectangular prism, V = lwh and SA = 2(lw + lh + wh). In 6, V = 8·9·20 = 1440 ft³ and SA = 2(72 + 160 + 180) = 824 ft². For 7, length factor 4 means surface factor 16 and volume factor 64.

For a closed cylinder, V = πr²h and SA = 2πr² + 2πrh. In 10, r = 3 ft and h = 8 ft. In 12, the 12 cm line extends from the edge to the center, so it is a radius, with h = 20 cm. In 13, halve both radius and height: surface area becomes one quarter and volume one eighth.

Conversions, sectors and arcs

Go heading, page 64

The student packet’s topic line says “Finding Centers of Rotation,” but questions 14–26 ask for degree/radian conversions, sector areas and arc lengths. Follow the numbered directions.

Multiply degrees by π/180 and radians by 180/π. For 18, keep the decimal input as given: 4.7 radians = 846/π degrees ≈ 269.290°.

For 23, a 100° sector with radius 13 cm has area (100/360)π·13² = 845π/18 cm². For 25, a 150° arc with radius 25 m has length (150/360)·2π·25 = 125π/6 m. Area uses r² and square units; arc distance uses r and length units.

Teacher/parent review of the source packet

The task intentionally contrasts interpretations of “twice as big.” From Main 4 onward, use a similarity scale factor of 2 for every length; the three castles have factors 1,2,4 relative to the smallest. Main 6 scales thickness too. Total surface area includes all bases, as the mold questions specify. The Go topic heading does not describe its numbered work; the clarification above identifies it.
