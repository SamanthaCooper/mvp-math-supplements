Section 7.1 · Main task · 1–5 · pages 1–4

Why a rotation center is equally far from corresponding points

A rotation turns a figure about a fixed point called its center. The distance from the center to each point stays unchanged. If \(A\) rotates to \(A'\), a possible center \(O\) must satisfy \(OA=OA'\).

The perpendicular bisector of a segment passes through its midpoint at a right angle. Its points are exactly the points equally far from the two endpoints. Therefore the possible rotation centers for one distinct pair \(A,A'\) lie on the perpendicular bisector of \(AA'\). The angle may be different for different centers.

Main 1–2 — Describe all possible centers

The packet's points are \(A=(4,3)\) and \(A'=(0,5)\). Their midpoint is \((2,4)\). Segment \(AA'\) has slope \(-\frac12\), so its perpendicular bisector has slope 2 and equation \(y=2x\).

Sample additional centers: \((1,2)\), \((3,6)\), and \((4,8)\). Each lies on \(y=2x\) and is equally far from \(A\) and \(A'\). Carlos's “any point” claim is too broad: any point on this perpendicular bisector works. Both the midpoint and the origin work too, even if neither was the center originally chosen.

Main 3–4 — Find a center for a whole figure

  1. Join two pairs of corresponding points, such as \(A,A'\) and \(B,B'\).
  2. Construct the perpendicular bisector of each joining segment.
  3. Use their intersection as the candidate center.
  4. Check another point pair and confirm the same turn carries every pre-image point to its matching image.

To construct a perpendicular bisector, open a compass to more than half the segment's length. Draw equal-radius arcs from its two endpoints. The line through the two arc intersections is the required bisector.

For the packet triangle, the center is \((2,1)\), and the turn from \(ABC\) to \(A'B'C'\) is \(90^\circ\) counterclockwise. For example, relative to this center, \(A=(6,5)\) has displacement \((4,4)\), which turns to \((-4,4)\), reaching \(A'=(-2,5)\). The other pairs give the same center and turn.

Why the method works: Each joining segment is a chord of a circle centered at the rotation center. The center is equally far from both ends, so it lies on each perpendicular bisector. Two distinct intersecting bisectors identify it. If two chosen bisectors coincide, use a different corresponding pair.

Main 5 — A chord's perpendicular bisector also bisects its central angle

A circle is the set of points a fixed distance from its center. A radius joins the center to the circle. A chord joins two points on the circle; a diameter is a chord through the center. A central angle has its vertex at the center, with sides along two radii.

Proof: Let \(M\) be the midpoint of a non-diameter chord \(AB\). The center \(O\) lies on the perpendicular bisector because \(OA=OB\). In triangles \(OMA\) and \(OMB\), the radii \(OA,OB\) are equal, \(MA=MB\), and \(OM\) is shared. The triangles are congruent by SSS, so \(\angle AOM=\angle MOB\). Thus the perpendicular bisector bisects the central angle. For a diameter, the perpendicular line through the center divides the straight central angle into two right angles.

Other circle words on page 3

Concentric circles share a center. A secant line meets a circle at two points; a tangent line meets it at one point. An arc is part of a circle. An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle and its sides along chords. Its intercepted arc is the arc between its endpoints that lies opposite the angle.

Student packet correction — page 3, “Inscribed angle”

The definition includes “or a secant and tangent line.” An angle between a tangent and a chord is a tangent–chord angle, rather than an inscribed angle. For an inscribed angle, use two chord rays meeting at a point on the circle.

4. Go

In your MVP packet

Section 7.1 · Go · 15–17 · page 7

For radius \(r\), circumference is \(C=2\pi r\) and area is \(A=\pi r^2\). Circumference measures distance around the circle, in length units; area measures the enclosed region, in square units. Keep exact multiples of \(\pi\) unless asked to round. Try the exercises first.
