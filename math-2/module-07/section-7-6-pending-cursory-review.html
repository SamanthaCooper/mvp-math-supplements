Find angles before lengths — Main 1

The radius is 5 cm, AB passes through center D, F bisects chord AE, and GE and GA are tangents. Start with the two given arcs: EB = 40° and BC = 60°. Since AB is a diameter, arc ACB is 180°, giving arc AC = 120°. The other semicircle gives minor arc AE = 140°.

The central angle EDA is 140°. The perpendicular from D to the midpoint F of AE splits it into two 70° angles. Thus triangle DFE is right with hypotenuse 5, angle EDF = 70°, and:

\[DF=5\cos70^\circ\approx1.710\text{ cm}\]\[EF=AF=5\sin70^\circ\approx4.698\text{ cm}\]

A radius is perpendicular to a tangent. Triangle DEG is therefore right at E. Its D angle is 70°, so DG = 5/cos 70° ≈ 14.619 cm and GE = GA = 5 tan 70° ≈ 13.737 cm. Since G,F,D occur in that order, GF = DG − DF ≈ 12.909 cm.

The diameter triangle ACB is right at C. Angle BAC intercepts the 60° arc BC, so it is 30°. In a 30°–60°–90° triangle the side lengths are in the ratio 1 : √3 : 2. With hypotenuse AB = 10 cm, BC = 5 cm and AC = 5√3 cm. In triangle EAB, EB = 10 sin 20° ≈ 3.420 cm; AE = 10 sin 70° ≈ 9.397 cm.

Main 1 checking inventory

All lengths below are in cm: DA = DB = DE = 5; AB = 10; BC = 5; AC = 5√3; EB = 10 sin 20°; AE = 10 sin 70°; AF = EF = 5 sin 70°; DF = 5 cos 70°; DG = 5/cos 70°; GE = GA = 5 tan 70°; GF = DG − DF.

Consecutive arcs in order E → B → C → A → E measure 40°, 60°, 120°, 140°. Add consecutive pieces for other arcs; subtract from 360° for the other route.

At A: ∠EAB = 20°, ∠BAC = 30°, ∠EAC = 50°, ∠GAE = 70°, ∠GAB = 90°, ∠GAC = 120°. At E: ∠AED = 20°, ∠DEB = 70°, ∠AEB = 90°, ∠GEA = 70°, ∠GED = 90°, ∠GEB = 160°. At B: ∠EBA = 70°, ∠ABC = 60°, ∠EBC = 130°. At C: ∠BCA = 90°. At D: ∠EDB = 40°, ∠EDF = ∠FDA = 70°, ∠EDA = 140°; DB and DA form a straight angle. At F all angles made by AE and GD are right angles. At G: ∠EGD = ∠DGA = 20° and ∠EGA = 40°. Point D lies on ray AB, so using D in place of B at A names the same angle.

Use the 30° angle bisector — Main 2

Main 2, page 37: the tangency statement is too broad

The student packet says “All circles are tangent to each other and to the sides of the equilateral triangle.” In the pictured configuration, each small circle touches two triangle sides and the large circle; the small circles do not touch one another, and no circle touches every side except the large incircle. The solution below uses the pictured tangencies.

Let the large radius be R and the small radius be 4. A center lying on a 60° corner’s angle bisector is radius/sin 30° = twice its radius from the vertex. Along the top corner’s bisector, CO = 2R and CR = 8 (here R in CR is the packet’s point label). External tangency says the distance between centers is R + 4. Thus:

\[2R-8=R+4\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R=12\text{ cm}\]

The large incircle’s radius is one third of the equilateral triangle’s altitude, so the altitude is 36 cm. Half of the triangle is 30°–60°–90°, giving side length 24√3 cm, about 41.569 cm. The small centers are 16√3 cm apart, about 27.713 cm, greater than the sum of their radii 8 cm.

Choose a conversion factor that cancels the old unit

For inches to feet, multiply by 1 ft/12 in. Thus 50 in × (1 ft/12 in) = 25/6 ft. For yards, multiply by 3 ft/yd; for miles, multiply by 5280 ft/mi.

Temperature conversion also includes a shift. Use C = (5/9)(F − 32), subtracting 32 before multiplying. To go the other way, solve for F: F = (9/5)C + 32. For example 20°C corresponds to (9/5)(20) + 32 = 68°F.

Separate fixed information from free positions

13: B is the center. The 110° angle ABC fixes arc AC at 110°. Arc AE is also 110°, so the remaining arc CDE is 140°. The drawing does not fix D’s position along that arc. Call arc CD = t degrees; then arc DE = 140 − t degrees. This describes every remaining arc without guessing from the picture. Every drawn inscribed angle is half its intercepted arc.

14: the two 80° central angles give the same arc JH in the congruent circles. Work within each circle separately: the left arc GH is 70°, so arc GJ through H is 150°; the right arc JI is 151°, leaving arc HI = 129°.

15–16: use the circles shown with centers J,K,L, each passing through the other two centers. Their radii equal the equilateral triangle’s side. On each circle, the six points occur in 60° steps:

  • Center J: H, G, F, K, L, I.
  • Center K: J, F, E, D, C, L.
  • Center L: I, J, K, C, B, A.

This is a compact way to label the whole figure: a central angle spanning k steps measures 60k degrees along that route, and an inscribed angle is half the intercepted arc that excludes its vertex. For example, on circle J, arc KL = 60°; ∠KHL = 30°, and ∠HKL intercepts arc HIL = 120° and is 60°. Only use an angle if its sides are present or you explicitly add them.

For 16, r = 5. An arc spanning k steps has length k·5π/3. A chord whose shorter arc spans one, two, or three steps has length 5, 5√3, or 10. These rules give all named chord and arc lengths without a long list of repeated values.

Check your work — 13–16

13: arcs AC = AE = 110°, CDE = 140°, CAE = 220°. ∠ACE = ∠ADE = 55°, ∠CAE = 70°; ∠BAE = ∠AEB = 35°; ∠BCE = ∠BEC = 20°. With arc CD = t: arc DE = 140 − t, ∠CAD = ∠CED = t/2, ∠DAE = 70 − t/2, ∠AED = 55 + t/2. Central ∠ABE = 110°, ∠CBE = 140°. Remaining arcs follow by sums/complements.

14: left arcs JH = 80°, GH = 70°, major GJ = 210° (minor GJ = 150°), major JH = 280°. ∠GJH = 35°, ∠JGH = 40°, ∠JHG = 105°; triangle JFH base angles 50°. Right arcs JH = 80°, JI = 151°, HI = 129°; ∠JIH = 40°, ∠JHI = 75.5°, ∠HJI = 64.5°; triangle JKH base angles 50°.

15: triangle JKL has three 60° angles. Each listed circle has six consecutive 60° arcs; longer arcs are sums of those pieces. Central angles equal their arcs, inscribed angles are half their intercepted arcs. The long collinear segments are HD, GB, and EA across the union; each circle’s own diameter is given by its opposite points (for example HK in circle J).

16: all three radii 5; each circle circumference 10π; each 60° arc 5π/3, 120° arc 10π/3, semicircle 5π. Corresponding chords 5, 5√3, 10. HD = GB = EA = 15, each made of three collinear radius-length segments. Other drawn full chords GK, HL, JE, LD, IB, AC have length 5√3. For any other named pair on one circle, use the step rule above.

Section 7.6 · Go · 17–18 · page 39

Area adds pieces; perimeter follows the outside

17: the straight-sided portion is a 10-by-5 rectangle plus a 2-by-2 rectangle. The semicircle’s diameter is 5 + 2 = 7 ft, so its radius is 3.5 ft. Area is 50 + 4 + (1/2)π(3.5)² = 54 + 49π/8 ft². The outside straight segments total 5 + 8 + 2 + 2 + 10 = 27 ft. Add only the semicircular arc, 7π/2 ft, to get the perimeter.

18: the rectangle is 35 by 40 cm. The attached right triangle has hypotenuse 40 cm and leg 24 cm, so its other leg is √(40² − 24²) = 32 cm. The two semicircle diameters are 35 cm and 32 cm. Their shared diameters and the rectangle–triangle shared side are inside the composite figure; exclude them from perimeter.

Check your work — 17–18

17: A = 54 + 49π/8 ft²; P = 27 + 7π/2 ft.

18: A = 35·40 + (1/2)24·32 + π(35² + 32²)/8 = 1784 + 2249π/8 cm². P = 40 + 35 + 24 + (35π/2) + (32π/2) = 99 + 67π/2 cm.
