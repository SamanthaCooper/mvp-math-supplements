In your MVP packetMain 1–7 · pages 51–54

Build exact coordinates from special triangles

A unit-radius spoke is the hypotenuse of a reference triangle. A 30°–60°–90° triangle has side ratio \(1:\sqrt3:2\). Divide by 2 to make the hypotenuse 1: the legs are \(\frac{1}{2}\) and \(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\). A 45°–45°–90° triangle with hypotenuse 1 has two legs \(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\).

In Main 1’s left diagram, angle BAC is 30°, ABC is 60°, AB=1, AC=\(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\), BC=1/2, and B is \((\frac{\sqrt3}{2},\frac{1}{2})\). In the right diagram, the angles swap: BAC=60°, ABC=30°, AC=1/2, BC=\(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\), and B is \((\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt3}{2})\).

Use the reference angle, then the signs

On the unit circle, the point is \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\). The horizontal coordinate determines cosine; the vertical coordinate determines sine. Use symmetry to carry the first-quadrant lengths into other quadrants.

Worked example — Main 3b

Original problem: Find \(\sin(\frac{5\pi}{6})\) exactly. This angle is 150°, in Quadrant II. Its reference angle is \(\pi-\frac{5\pi}{6}=\frac{\pi}{6}\). The point has a positive y-coordinate, so:

\[\sin\frac{5\pi}{6}=\frac12.\]

There is no need to turn the answer into a decimal.

Worked example — Main 5i

Original problem: Find \(\sin(\frac{11\pi}{4})\). Remove one full turn:

\[\frac{11\pi}{4}-2\pi=\frac{3\pi}{4},\qquad \sin\frac{11\pi}{4}=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}.\]

The terminal point is in Quadrant II, above the x-axis.

Use the correct angle unit in a motion model

A radius-one wheel with its center at water level starts at the rightmost point as the paddle emerges, then turns counterclockwise. For a rotation time T:

\[h(t)=\sin\left(\frac{360}{T}t\text{ degrees}\right)\quad\text{or}\quad h(t)=\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}t\right)\text{ in radians}.\]

These are the same motion, not different models. Main 6 has T=3: use \(\sin(120t^\circ)\) in degree mode or \(\sin(2\pi t/3)\) in radian mode. Main 7 has T=12: use \(\sin(30t^\circ)\) or \(\sin(\pi t/6)\). Heights are in meters and negative below the water level.

2. Ready — Coterminal angles

In your MVP packetReady 1–7 · pages 55–56

Work independently first. Use the answers below to check your work after you have tried each problem, or to help if you are stuck.

Coterminal angles end at the same ray. Add or subtract whole turns: 360° in degrees or \(2\pi\) in radians. To find the negative angle in Ready 1–5, subtract 360°.

Which negative angle? — Ready 6, page 56

For the negative coterminal angle reached by subtracting one turn, the positive angle plus the absolute value of that negative angle is 360°. With additional turns it can be 720°, 1080°, and so on. State which coterminal angle you chose.

4. Go — Find an angle from a ratio

In your MVP packetGo 23–28 · page 58

Work independently first. Use the answers below to check your work after you have tried each problem, or to help if you are stuck.

The stated range \(0^\circ\le\theta\le90^\circ\) selects the first-quadrant angle (including endpoints). Use inverse sine or inverse cosine in degree mode, then round to the nearest degree. For example, \(\sin\theta=0.82\) gives \(\theta=\sin^{-1}(0.82)\approx55^\circ\). These inverse-function buttons find angles; they are not reciprocals.
