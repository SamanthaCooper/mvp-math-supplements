Radians measure an angle using the circle itself

An arc is part of a circle’s circumference. Let s be its length and r the circle’s radius. The angle that sweeps out that arc has radian measure:

\[\theta=\frac sr,\qquad s=r\theta.\]

One radian is the angle that sweeps out an arc exactly one radius long. This is an arc length along the circle, not the straight-line distance between the arc’s endpoints. Because a complete circumference is \(2\pi r\), one full turn has measure \(2\pi r/r=2\pi\) radians. A half-turn is π radians, so \(180^\circ=\pi\) radians.

A larger circle has a proportionally longer arc for the same angle. Both s and r scale by the same factor, so their ratio—and the angle—stays unchanged.

Worked example — Two circles, the same angle

Example based on Main 3–4: Twelve evenly spaced stakes divide the circle into twelve 30° turns. The second stake is one step from the first.

\[\theta=\frac{2\pi}{12}=\frac\pi6.\]
\[s_{12}=12\left(\frac\pi6\right)=2\pi\text{ meters},\qquad s_{18}=18\left(\frac\pi6\right)=3\pi\text{ meters}.\]

The outer arc is longer, but both give \(s/r=\frac{\pi}{6}\). For the next stake, double the angle and each arc length.

Coordinates and arc length are different descriptions

For the twelve-stake plan, use the familiar 30° and 60° triangles to find the first-quadrant coordinates. Reflect them across the axes for the remaining positions. On the radius-12 circle, the 30° point is \((6\sqrt3,6)\); on the radius-18 circle it is \((9\sqrt3,9)\). The coordinate pair gives horizontal and vertical displacement; s gives distance traveled around the rim.

For stake j, counting the starting stake as j=1, the angle is \((j-1)\frac{\pi}{6}\), the inner arc length is \(2(j-1)\pi\), and the outer arc length is \(3(j-1)\pi\). The ratios s/r are equal at corresponding stakes. In the original one-radius arc-spacing proposal, the circumference contains about 6.28 radius-length arcs, regardless of radius; six whole steps leave a short final gap rather than dividing the circle evenly.

4. Go — Convert degrees and radians

In your MVP packetGo 14–25 · page 36

Work independently first. Use the answers below to check your work after you have tried each problem, or to help if you are stuck.

To convert degrees to radians, multiply by \(\frac{\pi}{180}\). To convert radians to degrees, multiply by \(180/\pi\). These factors come from \(180^\circ=\pi\) radians; they are not arbitrary rules.

\[54^\circ\cdot\frac{\pi}{180^\circ}=\frac{3\pi}{10}\text{ radians}.\]
