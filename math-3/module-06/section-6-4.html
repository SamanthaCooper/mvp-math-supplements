From a circular position to a repeating graph

A periodic function repeats its outputs at regularly spaced inputs. For a height graph, the horizontal axis is time and the vertical axis is height—not the horizontal position of the rider. At the rightmost point, a counterclockwise rider starts level with the center and moves upward.

\[h(t)=30+25\sin(18t^\circ).\]

The angle is measured in degrees. In one 20-second turn, five useful points are (0,30), (5,55), (10,30), (15,5), and (20,30). Join them with a smooth curve, not straight segments.

Three features to read before graphing

The midline is the horizontal halfway level between the maximum and minimum. The amplitude is the distance from that midline to either extreme, so it is nonnegative. The period is the input length for one complete cycle.

FeatureOriginal wheelWhat changes it
Midlineh=30 feetCenter height
Amplitude25 feetWheel radius
Period20 secondsTime for one revolution
\[h(t)=c+r\sin\left(\frac{360}{T}t\text{ degrees}\right).\]

Here c is the center height, r is the radius, and T is the positive rotation time. This rule assumes a counterclockwise start from the rightmost point. A clockwise start reverses the vertical displacement, so use a minus sign before the sine term. Faster rotation means a shorter period and a larger angular-speed coefficient.

Worked example — Main 8a

Original problem: Radius 30 feet, center 45 feet high, angular speed 15° per second counterclockwise.

\[h(t)=45+30\sin(15t^\circ),\qquad T=\frac{360}{15}=24\text{ seconds}.\]

The midline is 45, the amplitude is 30, and the heights range from 15 to 75 feet. Quarter-cycle points occur every 6 seconds.

Main 8b: for the specified clockwise wheel centered at ground level, \(h(t)=-50\sin(24t^\circ)\). It starts at height 0 and descends; its period is 15 seconds.

2. Ready — Even and odd symmetry

In your MVP packetReady 1–9 · page 21

An even function has y-axis symmetry: \(f(-x)=f(x)\). An odd function has 180° rotational symmetry about the origin: \(f(-x)=-f(x)\). A graph can be neither.
