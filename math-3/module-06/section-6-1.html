In your MVP packetMain 1–4 · pages 1–2

Find a height using a right triangle

For a refresher on sine, cosine and tangent in right triangles, see Math II 6.8: right-triangle ratios.

A wheel’s radius is the distance from its center to its rim. Here the radius is 25 feet and the center is 30 feet above the ground. A point’s height is the center’s height plus its vertical distance above the center, or minus its vertical distance below the center.

At the top the height is \(30+25=55\) feet; at the bottom it is \(30-25=5\) feet. At either side, the point is level with the center: 30 feet.

Drop a perpendicular from a point on the rim to the horizontal line through the center. This makes a right triangle with hypotenuse 25. If its acute angle at the center is \(\alpha\), then:

\[\sin\alpha=\frac{\text{vertical distance}}{25},\qquad \text{vertical distance}=25\sin\alpha.\]

Use degree mode on your calculator. At this stage, use acute triangle angles and symmetry; a general sine rule for all rotation angles comes later.

Worked example — Main 4, point B

Original problem: Find B’s height. The ten spokes divide a full turn into \(360^\circ/10=36^\circ\) intervals, so B is 36° above the rightward horizontal.

\[\text{vertical distance}=25\sin36^\circ\approx14.69\]
\[\text{height}=30+14.69\approx44.69\text{ feet}.\]

Point E is equally high by symmetry. Points G and J are the same vertical distance below the center, so their heights are \(30-14.69\approx15.31\) feet. For C and D use 72°, and reflect to obtain H and I.

2. Ready — Angular and linear speed

In your MVP packetReady 1–5 · page 3

Work independently first. Use the answers below to check your work after you have tried each problem, or to help if you are stuck.

Angular speed measures angle turned per unit of time. One revolution is 360°. Linear speed measures distance along the circle per unit of time. A point travels one circumference, \(2\pi r=\pi d\), per revolution.

\[\text{degrees per minute}=360\times\text{revolutions per minute}\]

4. Go — Connect the trig ratios

In your MVP packetGo 9–14 · page 4

Work independently first. Use the answers below to check your work after you have tried each problem, or to help if you are stuck.

For an acute angle in a right triangle, use \(\sin\theta=\text{opposite}/\text{hypotenuse}\), \(\cos\theta=\text{adjacent}/\text{hypotenuse}\), and \(\tan\theta=\text{opposite}/\text{adjacent}\). Use the Pythagorean theorem to find the missing side. For example, if sine is 4/5, choose opposite 4 and hypotenuse 5; the adjacent side is 3.
