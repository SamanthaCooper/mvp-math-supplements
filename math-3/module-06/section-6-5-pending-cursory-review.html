Cosine tracks horizontal position

We used sine to describe a point’s vertical coordinate. Cosine describes its horizontal coordinate. For a point \((x,y)\) on a circle of positive radius r centered at the origin:

\[\cos\theta=\frac xr,\qquad x=r\cos\theta.\]

This agrees with adjacent/hypotenuse in an acute right triangle. To the right of the center x is positive; to the left x is negative. At the top and bottom x is zero. Unlike height above the ground, the horizontal coordinate does not include the 30-foot center height.

Worked example — Main 3, 8 seconds

Original problem: Find the horizontal position 8 seconds after A. A full turn takes 20 seconds, so the rotation is \(18(8)=144^\circ\). Its reference angle is 36° and the point is left of center:

\[x=-25\cos36^\circ\approx-20.23\text{ feet}.\]

The negative result means 20.23 feet left of the point directly below the wheel’s center.

A cosine rule and its graph

\[x(t)=25\cos(18t^\circ).\]

In the packet’s overhead-sun model, the shadow has this same horizontal position. The cosine graph starts at its maximum, rather than at the midline:

Use the diameter (the top height for a wheel touching the ground), not the radius, in \(C=\pi d\). Divide a circumference by the number of equally spaced spokes to find the arc between consecutive spokes. One mile is 63,360 inches.

4. Go — Special triangles

In your MVP packetGo 13–25 · pages 28–29

Work independently first. Use the answers below to check your work after you have tried each problem, or to help if you are stuck.

Special triangles let you keep exact values. A 45°–45°–90° triangle has side ratio \(1:1:\sqrt2\); a 30°–60°–90° triangle has ratio \(1:\sqrt3:2\), with the shortest side opposite 30°.

Missing information — Go 21, page 29

The diagram marks only the two sloping sides equal and labels a side 16 feet. It does not label the base or an angle, or mark all three sides equal. That is not enough information to determine AD uniquely. If an equilateral triangle was intended, the diagram needs an additional mark; under that extra assumption AD would be \(8\sqrt3\) feet.
