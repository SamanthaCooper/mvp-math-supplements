In your MVP packetMain 1–7 · pages 15–16

Choose a model, then check its starting value

For a radius \(R\), center height \(D\), period \(T\) and counterclockwise initial angle \(\phi\), a height model is

\[h(t)=D+R\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}t+\phi\right).\]

Measure \(\phi\) counterclockwise from the rightmost point. Check both the initial height and whether the rider initially rises or falls. Those checks often catch a phase error.

Worked example — Main 1–3

Original problem: The rider starts at A, the rightmost point, on a radius-40-foot wheel with center 50 feet high and period 18 seconds. When is the height 70 feet?

Worked solution: Starting at A means \(\phi=0\), so

\[h(t)=50+40\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{9}\right).\]
\[70=50+40\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{9}\right)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{9}\right)=\frac12.\]

In the first revolution the angles are \(\pi/6\) and \(5\pi/6\), giving \(t=1.5\) and \(7.5\) seconds. These times repeat every 18 seconds. An equivalent cosine model is \(h(t)=50+40\cos(\pi t/9-\pi/2)\).

Read the initial angle from the diagram

The ten equally spaced positions are \(\pi/5\) radians apart, so D is at \(3\pi/5\). For Main 4 use \(h(t)=50+40\sin(\pi t/9+3\pi/5)\). To solve Main 5, isolate sine as \(3/4\), find both corresponding angles, add full turns as needed, then subtract the starting angle before dividing by \(\pi/9\). This produces about 1.17 and 15.03 seconds in the first revolution.

Main 7, page 16 — interpreting a height model

The equation you exchange comes from question 6. Height alone does not uniquely determine the rider’s direction or starting side: a suitable clockwise model can have the same height function as a counterclockwise model. Center height, radius and period can be read from a nonconstant sinusoidal height function; direction needs an additional convention or horizontal-position information.

Different functions do not simply cancel

For \(\sin(\arctan 2.75)\), let \(\theta=\arctan(11/4)\). Draw a right triangle with opposite 11, adjacent 4, and hypotenuse \(\sqrt{137}\). Then \(\sin\theta=11/\sqrt{137}\). This uses the meaning of the inverse rather than treating different trig names as canceling symbols.
