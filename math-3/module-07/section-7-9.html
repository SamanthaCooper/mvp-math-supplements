In your MVP packetMain 1–5 · pages 59–61

Write all solutions as repeating families

In a general solution, \(k\) represents any integer: 0, ±1, ±2, and so on. The expression \(a+kP\) gives every angle obtained by adding whole multiples of the repeat length \(P\) to a starting angle \(a\). Sometimes two or more families are needed.

Worked example — Main 1

Original problem: Solve \(2\sin x\cos x+\sqrt3/2=\sqrt3\).

Worked solution: Subtract \(\sqrt3/2\), then use the double-angle identity:

\[2\sin x\cos x=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sin(2x)=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.\]

The two angle families are \(2x=\pi/3+2k\pi\) and \(2x=2\pi/3+2k\pi\). Divide the entire expression, including its repeat term, by 2:

\[x=\frac\pi6+k\pi\quad\text{or}\quad x=\frac\pi3+k\pi.\]

Successive solutions in the combined list have alternating gaps; adding \(\pi\) repeats each family, rather than jumping to the next member of the combined list.

Main introduction, page 59; Set instructions, page 62

The packet calls the repeat term the “interval between successive solutions.” That need not be one fixed interval across all solutions. Write each family with its own starting angle and repeat length. This also avoids forcing every equation into one expression of the form “answer ± kπ.”

Worked example — Main 4: factor without losing a branch

Original problem: Solve \(2\cos^2x-\sqrt3\cos x=0\).

Worked solution:

\[\cos x(2\cos x-\sqrt3)=0.\]

Either \(\cos x=0\), giving \(x=\pi/2+k\pi\), or \(\cos x=\sqrt3/2\), giving \(x=\pm\pi/6+2k\pi\). Dividing by \(\cos x\) would lose the first family.

For Main 3, substitute \(s=\sin x\) to obtain \((s-2)(s+1)=0\); reject \(s=2\) because sine is between −1 and 1. For Main 5, expanding the square and using \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\) gives \(\sin2x=1/2\). These are algebraic equations in trig values before they become angle problems.

Check your work — Ready 1–6

1. \(ax+by=c\) exposes intercepts \((c/a,0),(0,c/b)\) when the respective denominator is nonzero; slope \(-a/b\) if \(b\ne0\). 2. \(m\) is slope, \(b\) the vertical intercept. 3. Slope \(m\) through \((x_1,y_1)\).

4. \(a\) determines opening and vertical scale; \(c\) is the vertical intercept; axis \(x=-b/(2a)\). 5. Roots \(r_1,r_2\), axis their average, with opening and scale determined by \(a\). 6. Vertex \((h,k)\), axis \(x=h\), opening and scale determined by \(a\). For the quadratic forms, \(a\ne0\).
