Tangent compares the two coordinates

On a circle of radius \(r\), a point at angle \(\theta\) has coordinates \((r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\). Dividing vertical by horizontal gives

\[\tan\theta=\frac{y}{x}=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta},\qquad\cos\theta\ne0.\]

Dividing both coordinates by the same radius does not change their ratio. This extends the right-triangle definition to angles in every quadrant. Tangent is positive when the coordinates have the same sign, and negative when their signs differ.

Why tangent has vertical asymptotes

As an angle approaches \(\pi/2\), cosine approaches zero while sine approaches 1. Their quotient becomes arbitrarily large in magnitude. At \(\pi/2\) itself, division by zero is undefined. The same happens at \(\theta=\pi/2+k\pi\), where \(k\) is any integer. Those vertical lines are vertical asymptotes.

A half-turn changes both coordinate signs, leaving their ratio unchanged; tangent’s period is \(\pi\). Its range is all real numbers. It is odd: \(\tan(-\theta)=-\tan\theta\).

Main 3–4, pages 22–23 — use signed values

The packet describes tangent using a segment’s “length.” An ordinary length cannot be negative. To extend this picture beyond acute angles, use a directed vertical coordinate on the tangent line: positive above the horizontal axis and negative below. The unsigned segment length gives only \(|\tan\theta|\).

Three reciprocal functions

Secant, cosecant and cotangent are defined by

\[\sec\theta=\frac1{\cos\theta},\qquad\csc\theta=\frac1{\sin\theta},\qquad\cot\theta=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}.\]

Secant is undefined where cosine is zero; cosecant and cotangent are undefined where sine is zero. Where tangent is defined and nonzero, cotangent also equals \(1/\tan\theta\). At \(\pi/2\), cotangent is 0 even though tangent is undefined; the cosine-over-sine definition handles this correctly.

Secant and cosecant have period \(2\pi\), and outputs at least 1 or at most −1. Cotangent has period \(\pi\), all real outputs, and decreasing branches between its asymptotes \(\theta=k\pi\). Secant is even; cosecant and cotangent are odd.

Worked example — exact values at 5π/6

Original problem (additional example): Find tangent, secant and cosecant at \(5\pi/6\).

Worked solution: The unit-circle point is \((-\sqrt3/2,1/2)\). Therefore

\[\tan\frac{5\pi}{6}=-\frac{\sqrt3}{3},\qquad\sec\frac{5\pi}{6}=-\frac{2\sqrt3}{3},\qquad\csc\frac{5\pi}{6}=2.\]
