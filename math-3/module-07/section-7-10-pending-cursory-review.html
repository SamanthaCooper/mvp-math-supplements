In your MVP packetMain 1–6 · pages 64–66

Polar coordinates give a direction and a signed distance

Rectangular coordinates \((x,y)\) give horizontal and vertical position. Polar coordinates \((r,\theta)\) give an angle from the positive horizontal axis and a signed distance along its ray. Positive angles turn counterclockwise. If \(r<0\), move \(|r|\) units in the opposite direction.

A point can have many names. The packet’s \((6,120^\circ)\), \((6,-240^\circ)\), and \((-6,300^\circ)\) all name the same point. Its rectangular coordinates are \((-3,3\sqrt3)\).

Convert using the circle coordinates

\[x=r\cos\theta,\qquad y=r\sin\theta.\]

For a rectangular point other than the origin, choose \(r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}>0\) and an angle in the correct quadrant. The ratio \(y/x\) helps find an angle when \(x\ne0\), but tangent alone cannot distinguish opposite quadrants.

Worked example — negative r

Original problem (additional example): Locate \((-4,\pi/3)\).

Worked solution: Face the ray at 60°, then move four units backward. Equivalently use \((4,4\pi/3)\). Converting confirms

\[x=-4\cos(\pi/3)=-2,\qquad y=-4\sin(\pi/3)=-2\sqrt3.\]

A polar curve uses the output as r, not as y

For Main 3, \(r=6\sin2\theta\), calculate the signed radius at each angle. From 0 to \(\pi/2\), the radius goes from 0 to 6 and back to 0, drawing one petal with its tip at \(\pi/4\). Negative radii in the next quarter-turn draw points on the opposite side. Continuing through a full turn draws four petals.

The rectangular graph \(y=6\sin2\theta\) is a wave with amplitude 6 and period \(\pi\). The polar graph uses the same numerical outputs as distances along rotating rays, producing a different shape.

Other curves in the task

For Main 5, the radius is zero when \(\sin\theta=-1/2\), at \(7\pi/6\) and \(11\pi/6\). Between them the radius is negative and draws the inner loop. Useful graphing checkpoints are zeros, extreme radii, symmetry and the sign of the radius. Do not connect points as though the horizontal coordinate were the angle.
