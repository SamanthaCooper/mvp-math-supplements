In your MVP packetMain 1–5 · pages 40–41

Simplify first; then find every allowed angle

An identity can turn an unfamiliar equation into a familiar one. After simplifying, find all angles in the requested interval, not just the angle returned by an inverse-trig button. Here Main uses \([0,2\pi]\), including both endpoints.

Worked example — Main 1

Original problem: Solve \(\cos(\pi/2-\theta)=1/2\) on \([0,2\pi]\).

Worked solution: The cofunction identity turns the equation into \(\sin\theta=1/2\). The unit circle gives

\[\theta=\frac{\pi}{6}\quad\text{or}\quad\theta=\frac{5\pi}{6}.\]

Alternatively, put \(u=\pi/2-\theta\). Then \(\cos u=1/2\) means \(u=\pm\pi/3+2k\pi\). Solve for \(\theta\) and keep the values in the requested interval. Using only \(u=\arccos(1/2)=\pi/3\) misses the second answer. A correct algebraic solution and a correct graph have the same solutions.

Worked example — Main 5: a doubled input

Original problem: Solve \(\sin(2\theta)=\sqrt2/2\) on \([0,2\pi]\).

Worked solution: As \(\theta\) runs from 0 to \(2\pi\), the input \(u=2\theta\) runs from 0 to \(4\pi\): two full turns. Thus

\[u=\frac\pi4,\frac{3\pi}4,\frac{9\pi}4,\frac{11\pi}4\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\theta=\frac\pi8,\frac{3\pi}8,\frac{9\pi}8,\frac{11\pi}8.\]

Divide every angle by 2. Restricting \(u\) to one turn would lose half the answers.

Keep the original domain

In Main 4, \(\cos\theta\tan\theta=\sqrt3/2\) simplifies to \(\sin\theta=\sqrt3/2\), but the original requires \(\cos\theta\ne0\). The solutions \(\pi/3,2\pi/3\) satisfy that restriction. Simplifying does not restore inputs excluded by the original expression.
