From a geometric model to weight

Split the nail into familiar solids: a cylinder for the head, a narrower cylinder for the shaft, and a cone for the point. Read each radius as distance from the axis, and each length as the difference between its endpoint coordinates. Add the three volumes; see 5.2: solids of revolution for a refresher.

The given density is weight per unit volume. Multiplying volume by \(4.67\text{ oz/in}^3\) gives ounces. Multiply by the number of nails, then divide by 16 to convert ounces to pounds. Let \(N\) be the number of nails and \(V\) the volume per nail in cubic inches:

\[\text{total pounds}=\frac{N(4.67)V}{16}\]

Invented example: a steel piece of volume \(0.2\text{ in}^3\) weighs \((0.2\text{ in}^3)(4.67\text{ oz/in}^3)=0.934\text{ oz}\). The cubic-inch units cancel. Now apply that conversion to your own nail model before checking below.

Identify the function type first. For a parabola, vertex form gives its turning point; intercepts give useful sketch points. In 14, the rule is linear. For a refresher, see 3.1: polynomial functions and end behavior. Try Go, then check.
