1. In class: A Develop Understanding Task

Revisit the ideas from class

This section explains some of the ideas you explored in class through the guided exercises. Use it alongside the “A Develop Understanding Task” portion of Section 5.6 in your MVP packet.

In your MVP packetSection 5.6 · Main 1–3 · printed pp. 29–30
Try the packet task on your own first

Attempt the task, then use the explanations and worked solutions below to check your reasoning or work through difficulties.

Make right triangles before using right-triangle tools

An altitude runs from a vertex perpendicular to the opposite side or its extension. It creates right triangles, where SOHCAHTOA and the Pythagorean theorem apply. Choose an altitude that connects the given information to an unknown. The same altitude can be calculated from either smaller triangle; set those expressions equal.

Main 1 — one worked altitude strategy

Original problem: find the missing measurements when \(A=40^\circ,B=65^\circ,a=10\). Draw \(CH\) perpendicular to \(AB\) on the packet diagram.

Worked solution: \(C=75^\circ\). In the two right triangles,

\[h=10\sin65^\circ=b\sin40^\circ\]
\[b=\frac{10\sin65^\circ}{\sin40^\circ}\approx14.10\]

The two base segments give \(c=AH+HB\):

\[c=b\cos40^\circ+10\cos65^\circ\]

Answers: \(C=75^\circ,b\approx14.10,c\approx15.03\) (side lengths in the same units as 10). Annotated sketch not shown: mark the perpendicular foot H, height h, and base pieces AH/HB.

For an obtuse triangle, some altitudes meet a side’s extension. Use the smaller right triangles’ acute angles and check whether segment lengths must be added or subtracted. The general laws will be formalized in 5.7; here the useful idea is the shared altitude. Try the remaining Main work before checking.

Main 1–3 — compact checks and sample relationships
MainCheck / relationships
1\(C=75^\circ,b\approx14.10,c\approx15.03\); see the worked strategy.
2For the acute triangle described in the task: \(A\approx81.66^\circ,C\approx63.34^\circ,c\approx12.46\). Drop an altitude from C to AB: \(h=13.8\sin35^\circ\), then \(A=\sin^{-1}(h/8)\) and \(c=8\cos A+13.8\cos35^\circ\).
3Sample relationships: choose H on AB and let \(AH=x,CH=h\). Then \(h=b\sin A=a\sin B\), \(x=b\cos A\), \(c-x=a\cos B\), \(b^2=x^2+h^2\) and \(a^2=(c-x)^2+h^2\). These express the same height and the two base pieces in different ways.

Main2’s acute-triangle context selects the acute inverse-sine angle. The three numerical givens alone also permit an obtuse A ≈98.34°, with C ≈46.66° and c ≈10.14. Main3 is open-ended; other correct relationships are possible. Altitude annotations for 2–3 are not shown: add H and label the perpendicular height on the packet.

3. Set — two views of the same height

In your MVP packetSection 5.6 · Set 6–7 · printed p. 32

Name the unknown horizontal distances first. Use tangent to express each view’s vertical height, then equate the expressions. Keep full calculator precision (at least four decimal places during work) and use degree mode. Try Set, then check.

Set 6–7 — equations and distances

6. Let x be the closer horizontal distance and d the mountain height in miles.

\[d=x\tan25^\circ=(x+6)\tan5^\circ\]
\[x=\frac{6\tan5^\circ}{\tan25^\circ-\tan5^\circ}\approx1.3857066\]

Answer: \(d\approx0.6462\) mi, or approximately 3411.75 ft using the unrounded height.

7. The packet places the perpendicular foot between the stations. Let x be its distance from Star Point; the other distance is \(30-x\).

\[d=x\tan34^\circ=(30-x)\tan14^\circ\]
\[d=\frac{30\tan34^\circ\tan14^\circ}{\tan34^\circ+\tan14^\circ}\approx5.46\text{ mi}\]

Answer: approximately 5.46 mi from the road.
