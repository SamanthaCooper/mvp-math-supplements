Follow the distance to the axis

A solid of revolution is the region swept out when a flat region makes a full turn around an axis of rotation. A point travels in a circle whose radius is its perpendicular distance from that axis. The region need not spin rapidly; one full turn defines the solid.

  • About the \(x\)-axis, vertical distances become radii; horizontal lengths become heights or lengths of the solid.
  • About the \(y\)-axis, horizontal distances become radii; vertical lengths become heights.
  • A segment reaching the axis sweeps out a filled disk. A segment stopping short of the axis sweeps out a ring, with an inner and an outer radius.
Main 3–4 — one worked comparison

Original problem: rotate the packet triangle with vertices \((0,0),(4,0),(4,2)\) about the \(y\)-axis and find its volume.

At height \(y\), the horizontal segment runs from \(x=2y\) to \(x=4\). Rotation leaves a conical space inside a cylinder, rather than filling a cone. The cylinder and removed cone both have radius 4 and height 2.

\[V=\pi(4^2)(2)-\frac13\pi(4^2)(2)=\frac{64\pi}{3}\]

Answer: \(\frac{64\pi}{3}\) cubic units. About the \(x\)-axis instead, the same triangle makes a cone with radius 2 and height 4.

Sketching a profile or a slice

For a solid’s profile, draw the axis and one side of its outline. Shade the region to be rotated: fill to the axis for a solid body; use a strip between inner and outer contours for a hollow wall. A section containing the axis shows the profile and its reflection. A section perpendicular to the axis shows a disk or ring. See 5.1: cross sections for a refresher.
