1. In class: A Practice Understanding Task

Revisit the ideas from class

This section explains some of the ideas you explored in class through the guided exercises. Use it alongside the “A Practice Understanding Task” portion of Section 5.8 in your MVP packet.

In your MVP packetSection 5.8 · Main 1–2 and frame investigation · printed pp. 42–44
Try the packet task on your own first

Attempt the task, then use the explanations and worked solutions below to check your reasoning or work through difficulties.

Area from two sides and the angle between them

Use \(K\) for area so it is not confused with angle \(A\). With side \(a\) as base, the perpendicular height is \(b\sin C\). Substituting into base-times-height gives

\[K=\tfrac12 ab\sin C\]

The angle must be included: C is between sides a and b. Rotating the labels gives \(K=\tfrac12 bc\sin A\) or \(K=\tfrac12 ac\sin B\). If the supplied angle is not between the two known sides, first find the information you need using 5.7’s sine/cosine laws.

One invented example — use the included angle

Example: two sides measure 8 cm and 5 cm, and the angle between them is 30°. Find the area.

\[K=\tfrac12(8)(5)\sin30^\circ=10\text{ cm}^2\]

Answer: 10 cm².

One inverse-sine answer can miss a triangle

With two sides and a nonincluded angle (SSA), a calculator supplies the acute inverse-sine angle. Its supplement may also work. Check both: each must leave a positive third angle and satisfy the given sides. This explains why the frame investigation can have two valid triangles. Keep unrounded values until the final measurements.

Wrong angle in Jabari’s formula — printed p. 44

The packet prints \(a^2=b^2+c^2-2bc\cos C\). Since a is opposite A, the correct formula is \(a^2=b^2+c^2-2bc\cos A\). Use the given \(A=40^\circ\) in Jabari’s quadratic.

Main — area checks and the two frame solutions

Main1. \(C=76^\circ\). Find \(c=12\sin76^\circ/\sin36^\circ\) with the Law of Sines; the altitude to c is \(h=12\sin68^\circ\). Thus \(K=\tfrac12 ch\approx110.20\) square units.

Main2. \(K=\tfrac12 ab\sin C\), \(K=\tfrac12 ac\sin B\), or \(K=\tfrac12 bc\sin A\). Each pairs two sides with their included angle.

Frame investigation — both students’ approaches

Given: a =10.00 m, b =15.00 m, A =40.0°. This investigation follows Main2 without a printed problem number.

Jumal: \(\sin B=15\sin40^\circ/10\). The acute calculator result gives the first row below. Also check its supplementary angle, giving the second row. Find \(C=180^\circ-A-B\) and \(c=10\sin C/\sin40^\circ\).

\[c^2-30(\cos40^\circ)c+125=0\]
\[c=15\cos40^\circ\pm\sqrt{100-225\sin^2 40^\circ}\]

2. Ready — rotational symmetry

In your MVP packetSection 5.8 · Ready 1–15 · printed p. 45

A pattern has rotational symmetry when a turn about its center makes it coincide with itself. If the whole spoke pattern repeats N times around the circle, the smallest positive turn is \(360^\circ/N\). Count pairs or groups together when their spokes differ. Ignore the center design as the packet directs. Try Ready, then check.

3. Set — choose an area formula

In your MVP packetSection 5.8 · Set 16–27 · printed pp. 45–47

For 16–21, use two sides and their included angle as above. For 22–23, the supplied side \(c\) is opposite C. The Law of Sines gives \(a=c\sin A/\sin C\); substituting into \(K=\tfrac12 ac\sin B\) gives

\[K=\frac{c^2\sin A\sin B}{2\sin C}\]

For 24–27, all three sides are known. Heron’s formula uses the semiperimeter \(s=(a+b+c)/2\) (half the perimeter):

\[K=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}\]

Find s, subtract each side from it, multiply the four factors, then take the square root. Check that each side is shorter than the sum of the other two. Try Set, then check; approximations below use two decimal places.

Look at the information pattern: SSS/SAS points to the Law of Cosines; a known opposite side–angle pair points to the Law of Sines. See 5.7: choosing the law for a refresher. Follow the packet’s instruction to choose only; do not calculate the triangle measurements.

Teacher/parent review of the source packet

Jabari’s approach, printed p.44: replace cos(C) by cos(A); the given angle is A=40°. Teacher notes wrongly suggest the sine-law approach gives a unique triangle; both inverse-sine branches give valid triangles. Their frame answers are labeled cm although the given sides are in meters. Ready counts whole repeated spoke pairs/groups where necessary, excluding the center details. Problems16–21/24–27 give no physical units, so their areas use square units.
