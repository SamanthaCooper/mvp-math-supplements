A cone with its tip removed

A conical frustum is the portion between two parallel circular cuts of a cone. With larger radius \(R\), smaller radius \(r\), and perpendicular height \(h\), its volume is

\[V=\frac{\pi h}{3}(R^2+Rr+r^2)\]

You can obtain this formula by subtracting the removed small cone from the original large cone. When \(R=r\), the formula gives a cylinder; when \(r=0\), it gives a cone.

Main 2 — one worked volume

Original problem: find the volume formed by rotating the packet trapezoid about the \(y\)-axis, and explain how the diagram helps.

Worked solution: the bottom edge extends 8 units from the axis, the top edge extends 7 units, and the vertical separation is 2. Thus \(R=8,r=7,h=2\).

\[V=\frac{2\pi}{3}(8^2+8\cdot7+7^2)=\frac{338\pi}{3}\]

Answer: \(\frac{338\pi}{3}\) cubic units. The horizontal distances give radii, and the vertical distance gives perpendicular height.

Approximate a curved profile

Join selected points on the curve with straight segments. Each segment rotates into a cone, cylinder, or frustum. Find each piece’s volume and add. For a closer fit to the outline, use shorter segments where its shape changes most. Your answer depends on the segments you choose; label it an approximation. See 5.2: radii and rotation if identifying the pieces is difficult.

3. Set — a pyramid on a frustum

In your MVP packetSection 5.3 · Set 9–14 · printed pp. 16–17

The monument combines a square frustum and a square pyramid. For face area, use height within the slanted face; find it with the Pythagorean theorem. For volume, use vertical height. A square frustum has \(V=\frac h3(a^2+ab+b^2)\), where \(a,b\) are the two square side lengths. Try Set, then check.
