Connect the pieces of a polynomial puzzle

Use the given information to choose a starting point:

  • From roots, write factors \(x-r\), repeating each factor according to its multiplicity. Multiply by the given leading coefficient.
  • With real coefficients, a nonreal root brings its complex conjugate. Multiply that pair first to form a real quadratic.
  • From standard form and a known root, divide out its factor and solve the quotient.
  • Use the leading term to determine the far ends, and use the constant term for the y-intercept.
  • For a sketch, mark real zeros and the y-intercept. Cross at odd multiplicities and touch at even multiplicities; then connect with a smooth curve consistent with the ends.

Review 3.7 multiplicity, 3.8 known-root division, and 3.9 end behavior (drafts pending human review). A sketch should agree with additional sample outputs; roots and far ends alone do not locate every turning point exactly.

Worked solution — Main 1

Original problem: Roots −2, 1, 1; leading coefficient −2; degree 3. Find factored/standard forms, end behavior and a graph.

\[f(x)=-2(x+2)(x-1)^2\]
\[=-2(x^3-3x+2)=-2x^3+6x-4.\]

Answers: Root −2 is simple; 1 is double. The y-intercept is \((0,-4)\). Negative cubic leading term means the left end rises and the right end falls.

\[x\to-\infty:\ f(x)\to\infty\]
\[x\to\infty:\ f(x)\to-\infty.\]
Worked solution — Main 2

Original problem: Roots \(2+i,4,0\); real coefficients, leading coefficient 1 and degree 4. Complete the puzzle.

Worked solution: The missing fourth root is \(2-i\). The complex pair contributes \((x-2)^2+1=x^2-4x+5\):

\[f(x)=x(x-4)(x^2-4x+5)\]
\[=x^4-8x^3+21x^2-20x.\]

Answers: Four simple roots: 0, 4, \(2\pm i\). Both ends rise because the leading term is \(x^4\). Only 0 and 4 give x-intercepts, and the graph crosses at both.

2. Ready — Cancel common factors with restrictions

In your MVP packetSection 3.10 · Ready 1–9 · printed p. 74

Cancellation is division by the same nonzero factor in numerator and denominator. Factor first, cancel complete factors, and keep every restriction from the original denominator. Do not cancel terms across addition. Review 3.4 Ready (draft pending human review).

Original example — cancellation preserves the original restriction
\[\frac{(x-2)(x+1)}{x-2}=x+1,\qquad x\ne2.\]

The simplified rule has a value at 2, but the original fraction does not. The restriction stays with the simplification.

4. Go — Build rules from roots and describe equal-step change

In your MVP packetSection 3.10 · Go 18–25 · printed p. 77

Multiply conjugate pairs first, then include the real factor and leading coefficient. For the rate-of-change blanks, use the equal-input-step changes studied in 3.1 (draft pending human review).

Worked solution — Go 19

Original problem: Write the polynomial in standard form with leading coefficient −1 and roots \(1,1+\sqrt3,1-\sqrt3\).

\[f(x)=-(x-1)(x-1-\sqrt3)(x-1+\sqrt3)\]
\[=-(x-1)((x-1)^2-3)\]
\[=-(x-1)(x^2-2x-2)=-x^3+3x^2-2.\]

Answer: \(-x^3+3x^2-2\). Keep the leading coefficient −1 throughout.

A fixed-step change has one lower degree

For unit steps, the change in output is

\[\Delta f(x)=f(x+1)-f(x).\]

For \(f(x)=ax^2+bx+c\), expansion gives \(\Delta f(x)=2ax+a+b\), a linear rule when \(a\ne0\). For a cubic, the leading change term is quadratic. In general, the highest terms \(a_nx^n\) cancel in the subtraction; the remaining leading term is \(na_nx^{n-1}\). Thus a nonconstant degree-n polynomial has a degree-\((n-1)\) fixed-step change. For another fixed nonzero step, the same degree conclusion holds, including when dividing by that step to get the average rate.

Variable repair — Go 21, printed p. 77

Read “a factor of \(f(b)\)” as “a factor of \(f(x)\).” The value \(f(b)=0\) is a number; the Factor Theorem identifies a factor of the polynomial.
