Multiply every term by every term

The distributive property, \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\), is the basis of the area and box models in the packet (Main 1 answer). A box records one product for each pair of terms, so nothing is missed. Multiply the coefficients, add exponents of the same variable, then combine like terms.

A product of factors is factored form. After multiplying and combining like terms, write standard form with powers in descending order. For a refresher on combining like terms, see Section 3.3, a draft pending human review.

Worked solution — Main 2(a)

Original problem: Multiply \((x+3)(x+4)\).

\[(x+3)(x+4)=x^2+4x+3x+12\]
\[=x^2+7x+12.\]

There are four pairwise products. For Main 2(b), the same method gives

\[(x+7)(x-2)=x^2+5x-14.\]

Worked solution: Add all six cells and combine matching powers:

\[(x+2)(x^2-3x+5)=x^3-x^2-x+10.\]

FOIL records four products for two binomials. This problem has six products, so a box or full distribution keeps track of the larger number of terms.

Main 4 — Check equivalent forms by graphing

Enter \(y_1=(x+2)(x^2-3x+5)\) and \(y_2=x^3-x^2-x+10\) in graphing technology. Their curves overlap. You can also compare selected outputs, such as 0 at \(x=-2\) and 10 at \(x=0\).

A graph is a useful error check. Distribution gives the algebraic equality for every input, even outside the viewing window.

Worked solution — Main 5: connect every pair of terms

Original problem: Use Tehani’s connecting strategy for \((x-3)(x^2+4x-2)\), then check by graphing.

\[x(x^2+4x-2)-3(x^2+4x-2)\]
\[=x^3+4x^2-2x-3x^2-12x+6\]
\[=x^3+x^2-14x+6.\]

The connecting arrows represent the same six products that a box would record. Plot not shown. Enter the original product and final polynomial together; their curves should overlap.

Build Pascal’s Triangle

In your MVP packetSection 3.4 · Main 7–12 · printed pp. 22–23

Main 7(a–b) answers:

\[(x+1)^2=x^2+2x+1\]
\[(x+1)^3=x^3+3x^2+3x+1.\]

For the cube, multiply the squared result by \((x+1)\) and combine like terms. The coefficients are \(1,3,3,1\). Plots not shown. Graph each original power with its expanded rule to check overlap.

Pascal’s Triangle organizes the coefficients of binomial powers. Begin with row 0 containing 1. Every row starts and ends with 1. Each interior entry is the sum of the two entries just above it.

Main 8 answer: the coefficients of the square and cube match rows 2 and 3. Main 9 answer: add neighboring numbers of \(1,3,3,1\) to create \(1,4,6,4,1\). For example, the middle entry is \(3+3=6\).

Packet references — Main 8 and 12

In Main 8 (printed p. 22), use the expansions from problem 7, rather than the printed reference to problem 5. In Main 12 (printed p. 23), put the answers into the table under problem 8, rather than the reference to problem 6.

Main 10 worked check: multiply \((x^2+2x+1)(x^2+2x+1)\), or multiply \((x+1)^3\) by \((x+1)\). Combining like terms gives

\[(x+1)^4=x^4+4x^3+6x^2+4x+1.\]

Main 11 answer: the coefficients agree with row 4. Make the next row by adding each neighboring pair, with 1 at each end.

Main 12 answers: row 5 is \(1,5,10,10,5,1\), giving

\[(x+1)^5=x^5+5x^4+10x^3+10x^2+5x+1.\]

Why the neighboring numbers are added

When multiplying an expanded rule by \((x+1)\), one copy is multiplied by \(x\), and another by 1. Matching powers receive contributions from neighboring terms in the previous expansion. Their coefficients add. For example, in \((x^2+2x+1)(x+1)\), the \(x^2\) terms are \(x^2\) from multiplying by 1 and \(2x^2\) from multiplying by \(x\), making \(3x^2\).

Use Pascal’s coefficients with powers of both terms

In your MVP packetSection 3.4 · Main 13–15 · printed p. 23

To expand \((u+v)^n\):

  1. Take the coefficients from row \(n\).
  2. Write powers of \(u\) decreasing from \(n\) to 0.
  3. Write powers of \(v\) increasing from 0 to \(n\).
  4. Multiply each coefficient by both powers, then simplify.

The powers in each term add to \(n\). For example:

\[(u+v)^3=u^3+3u^2v+3uv^2+v^3.\]

Main 13 answer: in \((x+a)^n\), powers of \(a\) increase from 0 to \(n\), while powers of \(x\) decrease. The correct fourth-power expansion is

\[(x+a)^4=x^4+4ax^3+6a^2x^2+4a^3x+a^4.\]
Packet correction — Main 13, printed p. 23, and Set table, printed p. 25

The printed fourth-power row has \(3a^3x\). It must be \(4a^3x\), matching Pascal’s coefficients \(1,4,6,4,1\). Using 3 gives an incorrect expansion.

For a direct check, set \(x=a=1\): \((1+1)^4=16\), while the printed expression adds to 15. Use the corrected row above.

Worked solution — Main 14

Original problem: Use Pascal’s Triangle to expand \((x+2)^3\), then check by multiplication.

Worked solution: Row 3 is \(1,3,3,1\). Keep the powers of 2:

\[(x+2)^3=x^3+3x^2(2)+3x(2^2)+2^3\]
\[=x^3+6x^2+12x+8.\]

Multiplication check: \((x+2)^2=x^2+4x+4\); multiplying that by \((x+2)\) gives the same four terms.

For a subtraction inside the binomial, treat the second term as negative. In \((x-2)^3\), use \(v=-2\); its odd powers are negative, so the signs alternate.

2. Ready — Divide out a known factor

In your MVP packetSection 3.4 · Ready 1–5 · printed p. 24

The packet’s long-division notation means \(f(x)\div g(x)\) or \(f(x)\div h(x)\). Division reverses multiplication. If \(f(x)=g(x)q(x)\), dividing by the nonzero factor \(g(x)\) leaves \(q(x)\).

Original example — divide a factored product
\[\frac{(x+4)(2x-1)}{x+4}=2x-1,\qquad x\ne-4.\]

Cancel the shared factor, not separate terms joined by addition. Keep the original denominator restriction.

For Ready 5, factor the quadratic first:

\[2x^2-5x+3=(x-1)(2x-3).\]

You can check by distribution: \(2x^2-3x-2x+3=2x^2-5x+3\). Then divide out whichever factor is named. Try the problems before checking.

Original example — the first term is also scaled

To expand \((2x+1)^3\), use \(u=2x\), \(v=1\), and row 3:

\[(2x+1)^3=(2x)^3+3(2x)^2+3(2x)+1\]
\[=8x^3+12x^2+6x+1.\]

The factor 2 is cubed in the first term and squared in the second. Do the same with the factor 3 in Set 17.

4. Go — Transform familiar parent graphs

In your MVP packetSection 3.4 · Go 18–26 · printed pp. 25–26

For \(y=aF(x-h)+k\), move a parent point \((u,F(u))\) to \((u+h,aF(u)+k)\). This rule works for lines, parabolas, square-root curves, exponential curves, and absolute-value graphs. For a refresher on shifts and vertical scaling, see Math II 2.1.

The parent \(y=x\) is a line; \(y=x^2\) is a U-shaped parabola; \(y=2^x\) is an increasing exponential curve; \(y=\sqrt{x}\) starts at \((0,0)\) and exists only for \(x\ge0\); \(y=|x|\) is V-shaped.

Worked solution — Go 23

Original problem: Graph \(f(x)=3\sqrt{x-2}\).

Worked solution: Move right 2 and multiply y-values by 3. Parent points \((0,0),(1,1),(4,2)\) become \((2,0),(3,3),(6,6)\). Start with a filled endpoint at \((2,0)\), then draw the increasing square-root curve through the other points. It becomes less steep as it moves right.

The domain is \([2,\infty)\), because \(x-2\ge0\). Plot not shown.

Try the graphs, then compare transformations and points. The key supplies points and important shape information; you still need to draw the requested plots.
