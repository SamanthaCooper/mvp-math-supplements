Use a known factor to reduce the problem

Knowing one factor lets you divide it out and work with a smaller expression. Main 1 answer: \(147\div7=21\), then \(21=3\cdot7\), so \(147=3\cdot7^2\). Main 2 answer: \(P(x)=(x-7)^2(x-3)\) similarly contains a repeated factor. Dividing out \(x-7\) twice leaves \(x-3\); its roots are 7 (multiplicity 2) and 3.

  1. A known root r gives factor \(x-r\); a known factor \(x-r\) gives root r.
  2. Divide by the known factor using polynomial long division.
  3. Factor the quotient or solve its quadratic equation.
  4. Include the original root and every root of the quotient, with multiplicities.
  5. Multiply the factors to check the original polynomial.

For a refresher, see 3.5 long division and 3.7 roots and factors (drafts pending human review). Keep both signs when taking a square root.

Main 7: After division, group the cubic quotient as \(x^2(x+2)+4(x+2)=(x+2)(x^2+4)\). Then \(x^2+4=0\) gives \(x=\pm2i\). You can also start with \(x^4-16=(x^2-4)(x^2+4)\).

Nonreal roots pair when coefficients are real

The Complex Conjugate Root Theorem says: if a polynomial has real coefficients and \(a+bi\) is a root, then \(a-bi\) is a root too. Conjugation reverses the imaginary part’s sign and preserves addition and multiplication. Conjugating \(P(a+bi)=0\) therefore gives \(P(a-bi)=0\), because the coefficients stay unchanged.

This lets you replace two complex linear factors with one real quadratic:

\[(x-(a+bi))(x-(a-bi))=(x-a)^2+b^2.\]
Worked solution — Main 8

Original problem: Find the remaining factors and roots of \(f(x)=x^3-x^2+4x-4\), given factor \(x-2i\).

Worked solution: Real coefficients force the conjugate factor \(x+2i\). Their product is \(x^2+4\). Dividing the original polynomial by it leaves \(x-1\):

\[f(x)=(x-1)(x^2+4)\]
\[=(x-1)(x-2i)(x+2i).\]

Answers: roots 1, 2i and −2i. Expand \((x-1)(x^2+4)\) to check all four original terms.

Main 9 answer: No real-coefficient polynomial can have exactly one nonreal root counted with multiplicity: its distinct conjugate must occur too, with the same multiplicity. Here the packet’s “imaginary” means nonreal complex.

This rational-coefficient condition is different from the real-coefficient condition for complex conjugate roots.

4. Go — Multiply radical and complex conjugate factors

In your MVP packetSection 3.8 · Go 15–20 · printed p. 57

Use the difference-of-squares identity \((A+B)(A-B)=A^2-B^2\). Keep parentheses around a compound A, and use \(i^2=-1\). Review 3.6 conjugates (draft pending human review).

Worked solution — Go 17

Original problem: Multiply \((x-4+2i)(x-4-2i)\).

\[(x-4)^2-(2i)^2=(x-4)^2+4\]
\[=x^2-8x+20.\]

Answer: \(x^2-8x+20\). The imaginary terms cancel; subtracting their square adds 4.
