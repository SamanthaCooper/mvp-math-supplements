Like terms and standard form

A term is a part separated by addition or subtraction, including its sign. Its numerical multiplier is its coefficient. Like terms have exactly the same variable powers: \(3x^2\) and \(-5x^2\) are like terms; \(3x^2\) and \(3x^3\) are not.

To add polynomials, combine the coefficients of like terms. Keep their exponents unchanged. To subtract, first change the sign of every term in the polynomial being subtracted, then combine like terms.

Write standard form with powers in descending order, for example \(2x^3-4x^2+7\). Missing powers have coefficient 0; you may leave a space for them when aligning columns.

For example, \(3x^2+5x^2=8x^2\), not \(8x^4\). Addition combines quantities with the same variable part; multiplying powers uses a different rule.

Main 1–4 — Why place-value columns help

In your MVP packetSection 3.3 · Main 1–4 · printed p. 13

Original problem 1: Compare 132 with \(x^2+3x+2\).

Answer: \(132=1(10^2)+3(10)+2\) has the same coefficient pattern as \(1x^2+3x+2\). The polynomial takes the value 132 at \(x=10\), but its value changes with \(x\); the number 132 is fixed.

Main 2 answer: The analogous polynomial for 2,675 is \(2x^3+6x^2+7x+5\).

Worked solution — Main 3

Original problem: Write the polynomial addition analogous to \(132+451\) and find its sum.

\[(x^2+3x+2)+(4x^2+5x+1)\]
\[=(1+4)x^2+(3+5)x+(2+1)\]
\[=5x^2+8x+3.\]

Main 4 answer: In both procedures, align matching places or matching powers and add their coefficients. Base-ten numbers may need carrying; polynomial coefficients do not have to stay below 10. For example, \(8x+7x=15x\) stays a term in \(x\).

Main 5(d): sum of two lines

At \(x=0\), the two outputs are 2 and 1; at \(x=2\), they are 4 and −1. The sum is 3 at each input, and the opposite slopes keep that sum constant.

Main 6 sample observations: Exponents stay the same when like terms are added. The result’s degree cannot exceed the higher input degree. If highest-degree terms cancel, the degree can decrease: the two lines in 5(d) add to a constant.

Subtract by adding the opposite

In your MVP packetSection 3.3 · Main 7–9 · printed p. 15

For any expressions \(A\) and \(B\), \(A-B=A+(-B)\). Multiplying an entire polynomial by −1 changes every term’s sign; this is the distributive property.

Worked solution — Main 7–8

Original problem 7: Write and subtract polynomials analogous to \(368-157\).

\[(3x^2+6x+8)-(x^2+5x+7)\]
\[=3x^2+6x+8-x^2-5x-7\]
\[=2x^2+x+1.\]

Main 8 answer: Yes: evaluating at \(x=10\) gives \(200+10+1=211\), analogous to the number result.

Main 9 answer: Yes, “change all the signs and add” is valid. It rewrites subtraction as addition of the additive inverse and distributes −1 over all terms. The packet’s worked example correctly gives \(-x^3+2x^2-9x-13\).

Main 10 — Track the order of subtraction

In your MVP packetSection 3.3 · Main 10–11 · printed p. 16
Worked solution — Main 10(b)

Original problem: Find \(f(x)-h(x)\), with \(f(x)=x^3+2x^2-7x-8\) and \(h(x)=4x^2-x-15\).

\[f(x)-h(x)=(x^3+2x^2-7x-8)-(4x^2-x-15)\]
\[=x^3+2x^2-7x-8-4x^2+x+15\]
\[=x^3-2x^2-6x+7.\]

The signs of all three terms of \(h\) change. In particular, subtracting \(-15\) adds 15.

ProblemAnswer
10(a)\(h(x)-k(x)=5x^2-8x-19\)
10(c)\(f(x)-g(x)=x^3+2x^2-3x-1\)
10(d)\(k(x)-f(x)=-x^3-3x^2+14x+12\)

Main 10(e): Read the two graph outputs and subtract in the printed order, \(l-m\). At \(x=0\), \(2-1=1\); at \(x=2\), \(4-(-1)=5\). Plot \((0,1)\) and \((2,5)\), then join with a line.

Main 11 sample reminders: (1) Subtract every term in the second polynomial. (2) Combine only like terms. (3) Rewrite subtraction as addition of the opposite to keep signs clear.

2. Ready — Distribute a monomial

In your MVP packetSection 3.3 · Ready 1–6 · printed p. 17

Multiply the term outside the parentheses by every term inside. Multiply coefficients and add exponents when multiplying powers with the same base.

Original worked example
\[3x^2(2x^3-x+4)=6x^5-3x^3+12x^2.\]

For the first term, \(x^2x^3=x^{2+3}=x^5\); for the middle term, \(x^2x=x^3\).

4. Go — Combine rational exponents

In your MVP packetSection 3.3 · Go 19–21 · printed p. 18

Same-base products add exponents, including negative exponents. A negative exponent represents a reciprocal: \(x^{-p}=\frac1{x^p}\). Find a common denominator and keep the negative sign. For 19–20, the even-root factors and negative exponents require \(x>0\). In 21 the root denominators are odd, so negative inputs are allowed; only \(x=0\) is excluded.

Try each expression, then check the exponent arithmetic.
