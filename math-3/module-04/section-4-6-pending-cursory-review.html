A reliable sketching process

A sign chart records where a function is positive or negative. It helps you determine which way each branch heads near a vertical asymptote. Combine it with intercepts, end behavior, and a few points; a sign chart alone does not locate every turn.

  1. Record the original excluded inputs, including zeros of an expression you divide by. Combine and factor the expression, then cancel common factors. Keep the original exclusions.
  2. At each excluded input, check the reduced denominator: a remaining zero gives a vertical asymptote; a finite reduced value gives a hole.
  3. Find horizontal or slant end behavior. Lower numerator degree gives \(y=0\); equal degrees give the leading-coefficient ratio. If the numerator degree is exactly one higher, polynomial division gives a slant asymptote.
  4. Find numerator zeros that are allowed inputs for the \(x\)-intercepts. Substitute \(x=0\) for the \(y\)-intercept when allowed.
  5. Split a number line at zeros, poles, and holes. Test one input in each interval and determine each factor’s sign. A rational function cannot change sign within such an interval.
  6. Near a vertical asymptote the reduced numerator stays nonzero while the denominator approaches zero. A positive quotient grows toward \(+\infty\); a negative quotient falls toward \(-\infty\). Plot extra points to guide branch shape.
  7. Sketch without joining across excluded inputs. Check the Main graphs with technology only after your hand sketch; refine your strategy.

For refreshers, see 4.4: holes and polynomial division and 4.5: combining rational expressions.

2. Ready — checking rational-equation solutions

In your MVP packetSection 4.6 · Ready 1–6 · printed p. 42

Check candidates in the original equation

An extraneous solution is a candidate from the algebra that fails the original equation. Start by excluding denominator zeros. Then multiply every term on both sides by the least common denominator, solve the resulting equation, and substitute the allowed candidates into the original equation. Multiplication by a nonzero common denominator preserves equality; a candidate where it is zero must be rejected.

Ready 5 — Original problem and worked solution
\[\frac1{x^2-x}-\frac1{x-1}=\frac12\]

Exclude \(x=0,1\). Multiply both sides by \(2x(x-1)\):

\[2-2x=x(x-1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2+x-2=(x+2)(x-1)=0\]

The candidates are \(x=-2,1\). Reject 1 because it makes an original denominator zero. At −2 the left side is \(\frac16+\frac13=\frac12\), so the solution is \(x=-2\).

Teacher/parent review of the source packet

No substantial mathematical error was found in the student exercises. Ready introduces rational-equation checking before 4.7’s main equation lesson, so a short solving procedure is supplied here. Set duplicates Ready problem numbers 5–6; component labels distinguish them.

The teacher-note summary (PDF p. 66) says any higher numerator degree gives a slant asymptote. Qualify this: a linear slant asymptote requires a degree difference of exactly one. The assigned improper expressions meet that condition.
