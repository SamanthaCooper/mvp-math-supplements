A ratio of polynomials

A rational function can be written \(f(x)=\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\), where P and Q are polynomials and Q is not the zero polynomial. Real inputs that make Q(x)=0 are excluded. The word “rational” describes the rule as a ratio of polynomials; it does not require every output to be a rational number.

The degree of a nonzero polynomial is its largest exponent after like terms have been combined. A nonzero constant such as 1 has degree 0. In a product, add factor degrees and multiply leading coefficients: \((2x+5)(2x-5)=4x^2-25\) has degree 2 and leading coefficient 4.

Review 3.9: polynomial end behavior (draft pending human review) for why leading terms control large-input behavior.

Why the degrees predict the horizontal asymptote

At large positive or negative inputs, the highest-degree terms dominate. Compare those terms in the fraction.

If the numerator’s degree is smaller, the denominator eventually grows faster in magnitude, so \(f(x)\to0\) at both ends. The horizontal asymptote is \(y=0\).

If the degrees are equal, the same power of x appears in both leading terms and cancels in their ratio. The horizontal asymptote is the ratio of leading coefficients:

\[y=\frac{\text{leading coefficient of }P}{\text{leading coefficient of }Q}.\]

Main 2 example: \(\frac{x}{(x+2)(x-3)}=\frac{x}{x^2-x-6}\). Divide the numerator and denominator by \(x^2\) to see the end behavior:

\[\frac{\frac1x}{1-\frac1x-\frac6{x^2}}\to\frac01=0.\]

Main 6 example: the numerator has leading term \(2x^2\); the denominator’s leading term is \(4x^2\), giving \(y=\frac24=\frac12\). More explicitly:

\[\frac{2x^2+3}{4x^2-25}=\frac{2+\frac3{x^2}}{4-\frac{25}{x^2}}\to\frac24.\]

The horizontal asymptote is determined by end behavior. It can be crossed at finite inputs, as Main 2 does at (0,0).

Find vertical asymptotes and intercepts

  1. Domain: exclude real zeros of the original denominator.
  2. Vertical asymptotes in these examples: set each denominator factor equal to zero. The numerator is nonzero at those inputs, so outputs grow without bound near them. An excluded input alone does not always establish a vertical asymptote; if both numerator and denominator are zero, examine the expression further before applying this shortcut.
  3. x-intercepts: solve P(x)=0 and keep only inputs in the domain. Write each as (x,0).
  4. y-intercept: substitute x=0, if allowed, and write (0,f(0)).
Worked solution — Main 4

Original problem: For \(y=\frac{3x-1}{x+4}\), find degrees, asymptotes and intercepts, then use technology to graph it.

Worked solution: both polynomials have degree 1, so the horizontal asymptote is y=3. The denominator is zero at −4, where the numerator equals −13; the vertical asymptote is x=−4. Set the numerator to zero: \(3x-1=0\Rightarrow x=\frac13\). Substitute zero for x to get \(-\frac14\).

Answers: degrees (1,1); asymptotes x=−4, y=3; x-intercept \((\frac13,0)\), y-intercept \((0,-\frac14)\). Check the graph against these algebraic features.

2. Ready — Add and multiply numerical fractions

For addition, use a common denominator so the parts have the same size, then add numerators. For multiplication, multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators; common factors can be divided out before or after. Keep fractions in lowest terms. Try the exercises before checking the steps.
