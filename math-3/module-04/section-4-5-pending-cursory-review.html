Use the same fraction rules with polynomial expressions

A rational expression is a ratio of polynomials. Its original denominator must be nonzero. Before an operation, record every excluded input; after simplification, keep those restrictions. Review 4.4: canceling factors and retaining the original domain (draft pending human review).

For polynomial expressions A, B, C and D, the rules are:

OperationRuleConditions
Multiply\(\frac AB\cdot\frac CD=\frac{AC}{BD}\)\(B\ne0,\ D\ne0\)
Divide\(\frac AB\div\frac CD=\frac AB\cdot\frac DC=\frac{AD}{BC}\)\(B\ne0,\ D\ne0,\ C\ne0\)
Add / subtract with like denominators\(\frac AB\pm\frac CB=\frac{A\pm C}{B}\)\(B\ne0\)

For division, the divisor must be both defined and nonzero. Its numerator becoming a denominator after reciprocation explains the extra restriction C≠0. Cancellation removes common factors of a full numerator and denominator, not pieces of sums.

Multiply, factor and cancel

In your MVP packetSection 4.5 · Main 1–2 · printed p. 29

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together. Keep the result factored so common factors are visible. Simplifying before multiplying usually keeps the work smaller; simplifying afterward gives the same result on the original domain.

Worked solutions — Main 1, expressions A and B

Original problems:

\[\text{A: }\frac{x(x-3)}{x+1}\cdot\frac5{x^2}\]
\[\text{B: }\frac{(x+1)(x-2)}{x+2}\cdot\frac{x+5}{(x-2)(x+2)}.\]

Worked solutions / table steps:

Missing procedure rowExpression AExpression B
Multiply numerators; multiply denominators\(\frac{5x(x-3)}{x^2(x+1)}\)\(\frac{(x+1)(x-2)(x+5)}{(x+2)(x-2)(x+2)}\)
Divide out common factors\(\frac{x}{x}\cdot\frac{5(x-3)}{x(x+1)}\)\(\frac{x-2}{x-2}\cdot\frac{(x+1)(x+5)}{(x+2)^2}\)

Answers: A gives \(\frac{5(x-3)}{x(x+1)}\), x≠0,−1. B gives \(\frac{(x+1)(x+5)}{(x+2)^2}\), x≠−2,2. The packet supplies these final forms; the missing rows show how to reach them. The numerical model is \(\frac34\cdot\frac56=\frac{15}{24}=\frac58\).

Main 2 answer: either order works. Dividing out a common nonzero factor does not change the ratio, so multiplying first or canceling first gives equivalent results. Factoring first helps prevent invalid cancellation.

Divide by multiplying by the reciprocal

In your MVP packetSection 4.5 · Main 3–4 · printed p. 30
Worked solutions — Main 3, expressions A and B

Original problems:

\[\text{A: }\frac{x-2}{x+2}\div\frac{x+5}{x(x+2)}\]
\[\text{B: }\frac{(x+1)(x-6)}{x+2}\div\frac{x+1}{(x-3)(x+2)}.\]

Worked solutions: the first missing procedure is “multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor”; the last is “write the simplified form.”

Missing expression rowAB
Replace division with reciprocal multiplication\(\frac{x-2}{x+2}\cdot\frac{x(x+2)}{x+5}\)\(\frac{(x+1)(x-6)}{x+2}\cdot\frac{(x-3)(x+2)}{x+1}\)
Multiply and cancel full common factors\(\frac{x(x-2)(x+2)}{(x+2)(x+5)}\)\(\frac{(x+1)(x-6)(x-3)(x+2)}{(x+2)(x+1)}\)

Answers: A: \(\frac{x(x-2)}{x+5}\), x≠−2,0,−5. B: \((x-3)(x-6)\), x≠−2,3,−1. Original denominator zeros remain excluded, and the divisor-zero inputs −5 and −1 are excluded too. When all denominator factors cancel in B, the remaining denominator is 1. The numerical model is \(\frac34\div\frac56=\frac34\cdot\frac65=\frac9{10}\).

Main 4 sample answer: check by evaluating the original operation and the simplified rule at an allowed input. For Main 3B at x=0, \(-3\div(-\frac16)=18\), agreeing with \((-3)(-6)=18\). A numerical check can catch an error; factor expansion or reversing the operation verifies an identity for all allowed inputs. Always compare restrictions too.

Add using a common denominator

In your MVP packetSection 4.5 · Main 5–8 · printed pp. 31–32

Fractions can be added when they refer to the same size of part. For polynomial fractions, rewrite both using a common denominator. Multiply each numerator and denominator by the same missing factor, so each fraction keeps its value.

  1. Factor the denominators and record their zeros.
  2. Build the least common denominator (LCD) using each distinct factor to the greatest power needed by either denominator.
  3. Multiply each fraction’s numerator and denominator by its missing factors.
  4. Add the full numerators; keep the common denominator.
  5. Factor the new numerator and cancel any full common factors, keeping exclusions.

For example, denominators \((x-2)(x+2)\) and x+2 need LCD \((x-2)(x+2)\), because x+2 already occurs in it. You do not need a second copy of that factor.

Worked solution — Main 5, expression A

Original problem: complete the addition organizer for \(\frac3{x+7}+\frac4{x-4}\).

Worked solution: the packet already shows multiplying each fraction by its missing factor. Fill the next two rows as follows:

Missing procedureMissing expression
Write equivalent fractions with the common denominator\(\frac{3(x-4)}{(x+7)(x-4)}+\frac{4(x+7)}{(x+7)(x-4)}\)
Add numerators; keep the denominator\(\frac{3(x-4)+4(x+7)}{(x+7)(x-4)}\)

Answer: distribute to get \(3x-12+4x+28=7x+16\), so the supplied final form is \(\frac{7x+16}{(x+7)(x-4)}\), x≠−7,4. The numerical model is \(\frac23+\frac17=\frac{14}{21}+\frac3{21}=\frac{17}{21}\).

Main 6 answer: yes, on the original domain. Each added multiplier is a nonzero expression divided by itself, hence 1. The fractions’ values remain unchanged while their denominators become the same.

Worked solution — Main 7, expression B

Original problem: complete the organizer for \(\frac{2x-3}{x+3}+\frac{x+5}{x-2}\).

Procedure rowExpression
Determine missing factors\(\frac{2x-3}{x+3}\cdot\frac{x-2}{x-2}+\frac{x+5}{x-2}\cdot\frac{x+3}{x+3}\)
Write equivalent fractions with the LCD\(\frac{(2x-3)(x-2)}{(x+3)(x-2)}+\frac{(x+5)(x+3)}{(x+3)(x-2)}\)
Add full numerators\(\frac{(2x-3)(x-2)+(x+5)(x+3)}{(x+3)(x-2)}\)

Worked solution: expand the numerator:

\[(2x^2-7x+6)+(x^2+8x+15)=3x^2+x+21.\]

Answer: \(\frac{3x^2+x+21}{(x-2)(x+3)}\), x≠2,−3. The organizer’s two blank procedure descriptions are the middle two rows above; its final simplified form is supplied.

Main 8 answer: yes. After addition, factor the combined numerator and denominator to look for common factors. Invented example:

\[\frac1{x-1}+\frac{x-2}{x-1}=\frac{x-1}{x-1}=1,\qquad x\ne1.\]

Cancel only after combining the numerator; the original exclusion stays.

Subtract the entire second numerator

In your MVP packetSection 4.5 · Main 9–10 · printed p. 33

Find an LCD as for addition. Then put parentheses around the second numerator: subtracting it changes the sign of every term. In general, \(\frac AB-\frac CD=\frac{AD-CB}{BD}\) when B and D are nonzero; using an LCD may give a smaller denominator than BD.

Worked solution — Main 9, expression A

Original problem: complete the subtraction organizer for \(\frac{3x+1}{x+5}-\frac{x-4}{x-2}\).

Missing procedure / stepMissing expression
Multiply by missing factors to obtain a common denominator\(\frac{3x+1}{x+5}\cdot\frac{x-2}{x-2}-\frac{x-4}{x-2}\cdot\frac{x+5}{x+5}\)
Write equivalent fractions with the LCD\(\frac{(3x+1)(x-2)}{(x+5)(x-2)}-\frac{(x-4)(x+5)}{(x+5)(x-2)}\)
Subtract full numerators; keep the denominator\(\frac{(3x+1)(x-2)-(x-4)(x+5)}{(x+5)(x-2)}\)

Worked solution:

\[(3x^2-5x-2)-(x^2+x-20)=2x^2-6x+18.\]

Answer: \(\frac{2(x^2-3x+9)}{(x+5)(x-2)}\), x≠−5,2. The numerical model is \(\frac78-\frac35=\frac{35}{40}-\frac{24}{40}=\frac{11}{40}\).

Wording repair — Main 9, printed p. 33

The instruction says to fill the steps for “adding.” Here the operation is subtraction, as shown in both original expressions.

Main 10 sample answer: put each compound numerator in parentheses. Expand each product separately, then distribute the minus sign to every term of the second product before combining like terms. In Main 9, subtracting \(x^2+x-20\) produces \(-x^2-x+20\).

2. Ready — Review right-triangle ratios

In your MVP packetSection 4.5 · Ready 1–4 · printed p. 34

For a refresher, see Math II 6.8: sine, cosine and tangent. Relative to the chosen acute angle, \(\sin=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\), \(\cos=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\), and \(\tan=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\). The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. Switching from A to B exchanges the legs’ roles. Try Ready before checking.

Inconsistent side lengths — Ready 2, printed p. 34

If the legs are correct, change the hypotenuse to \(\sqrt3\) cm. The key gives ratios for that correction and a second possible correction; the printed data do not determine which label was intended.

Set 5 — Angela’s work

5c is correct. She multiplies the fractions, cancels the common x−2 factor and expands the remaining product. The result is \(\frac{x^2+6x+5}{x^2+4x+4}\), x≠−2,2.

5a is incorrect: the first common-denominator line is correct, but she distributes incorrectly. \(5x(x-1)=5x^2-5x\) and \(2(x-3)=2x-6\), so

\[\frac{5x}{x-3}+\frac2{x-1}=\frac{5x^2-3x-6}{(x-3)(x-1)},\quad x\ne3,1.\]

\[\frac{x}{x+3}-\frac{4(x+3)}{x-1}=\frac{x(x-1)-4(x+3)^2}{(x+3)(x-1)}\]
\[=\frac{-3x^2-25x-36}{(x+3)(x-1)},\quad x\ne-3,1.\]

Set 12 — Comparable statements

a) Polynomials are rational expressions with denominator 1.

b) Rational-number fractions are undefined when their denominator equals zero.

c) Adding, subtracting or multiplying rational expressions, or dividing by a nonzero rational expression, produces a rational expression wherever the original operation is defined.

d) A rational expression is proper when the numerator polynomial’s degree is less than the denominator polynomial’s degree.

Division qualification — Set 12, printed p. 36

The supplied statement about rational numbers includes division without excluding a zero divisor. Add “provided the divisor is nonzero.” For example, \(\frac12\div0\) is undefined. The same qualification belongs in your rational-expression statement in c).
