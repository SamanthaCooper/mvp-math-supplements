Math Supplements

Step-by-step companion lessons for students using the Mathematics Vision Project curriculum.

Why this site exists

The Mathematics Vision Project (MVP) curriculum is discovery-based. Students work through tasks in class and are expected to figure out the mathematical ideas themselves. The packets contain little direct instruction and almost no worked examples, and the Ready, Set, Go homework has no answers to check against. Students who get stuck at home can have a hard time knowing how to move forward.

These lessons are an optimistic attempt to fill that gap. Each one follows a numbered MVP section and explains its mathematics directly, with clear explanations and step-by-step worked examples.

What’s in each lesson

  • Explanations of the section’s new ideas, one at a time, with new vocabulary defined where it is first used.
  • Fully worked examples that show every step, plus accurate graphs and diagrams where they help.
  • Help with Ready, Set, and Go, including review skills that the homework uses but the main task doesn’t teach.
  • Answer keys for Ready, Set, and Go, with numerical results and explanations where needed. Some lessons also include extra Go practice with worked solutions.
  • Packet clarifications: shaded boxes that explain confusing wording, unstated assumptions, or errors in specific packet problems.
  • Links to other instructional resources, where useful, such as Paul’s Online Math Notes, Khan Academy, and Professor Leonard. Each link points to a specific reading, video, or practice set, with a note on which parts match the lesson and which to skip.
  • For teachers and parents: a short review of serious problems in the packet, and a table showing where each homework skill is covered.

How to use these lessons

  • Use each lesson alongside its MVP section. Every lesson matches one numbered section of the packet. Read it on the day that section is taught: after class, or when you sit down to do the Ready, Set, Go homework.
  • Keep using the MVP packet. These lessons are a companion to the packet, not a replacement for its tasks and homework.
  • Try your homework before checking the answers. Work through the problems on your own, then use the provided answer key to check your work.
  • If you’re still confused, look for additional learning resources linked in the lesson.
