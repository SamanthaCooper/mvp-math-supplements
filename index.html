Why this site exists

The Mathematics Vision Project (MVP) curriculum is discovery-based. In class, students work through tasks and are expected to figure out the mathematical ideas themselves. The packets contain very little direct instruction and almost no worked examples, and the Ready, Set, Go homework has no answers to check against. A student who gets stuck at home often has nothing to turn to until the next day in class.

These lessons are meant to fill that gap. Each one teaches the same mathematics as the matching MVP section, but explains it directly, the way a traditional textbook would: clear explanations, rules, and step-by-step worked examples.

What’s in each lesson

  • Explanations of the section’s new ideas, one at a time, with new vocabulary defined where it is first used.
  • Fully worked examples that show every step, plus accurate graphs and diagrams where they help.
  • Help with all of Ready, Set, Go, including review skills that the homework uses but the main task doesn’t teach.
  • Extra practice problems with full solutions, modeled on the Go homework.
  • Packet clarifications: shaded boxes that explain confusing wording, unstated assumptions, or errors in specific packet problems.
  • Links to other instructional resources such as Paul’s Online Math Notes, Khan Academy, and Professor Leonard. Each link points to a specific reading, video, or practice set, with a note on which parts match the lesson and which to skip.
  • For teachers and parents: a short review of serious problems in the packet, and a table showing where each homework skill is covered.

How to use these lessons

  • Use each lesson alongside its MVP section. Every lesson matches one numbered section of the packet. Read it on the day that section is taught: after class, or when you sit down to do the Ready, Set, Go homework.
  • You won’t need to read ahead. Each lesson uses only ideas from its own section and earlier ones, so it makes sense even if your class hasn’t reached the next section yet.
  • It’s a companion, not a replacement. Keep doing the packet tasks and homework. The lessons are not an answer key; the worked examples are new problems that teach the same methods.
  • Stay online while reading. Equations are displayed using an online service, so they need an internet connection to appear correctly. Lessons are also formatted to print cleanly.
