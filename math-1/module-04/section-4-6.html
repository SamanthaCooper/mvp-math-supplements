In your MVP packetSection 4.6 · Main task · Problems 1–10 · pp. 24–26

The packet asks you to evaluate Joaquin’s and Serena’s reasoning. For a refresher on solving inequalities, see Section 4.4: Keep the comparison true.

Read the words and symbols carefully — Main 1–4

“6 less than a number” means subtract 6 from the number: \(n-6\). Similarly, “4 greater than x” means \(x+4\). An expression describes a quantity; an inequality compares quantities.

Main problemAnswer and reason
1Serena is correct. \(x\ge-7\) includes −7, so use a closed dot at −7 and shade to the right.
2Serena is not correct. For example, \(x=-1\) gives \(3(-1)+1=-2\), which is not greater than zero. Solving \(3x+1>0\) gives \(x>-\frac13\).
3Serena is correct: “4 greater than x” is \(4+x\). Joaquin’s \(4>x\) says that 4 is greater than x, a different statement.
4Joaquin is not correct. \(45+47<t\) means \(92<t\), or \(t>92\). When you exchange the sides of an inequality, reverse the symbol to keep the same comparison.

Worked example 1 — Compare an equation with an inequality

Original problem — Main 5: Compare solving \(-\frac{x}{3}=4\) with solving \(-\frac{x}{3}>4\).

Worked solution: Multiply both sides by −3 in each problem. In the inequality, reverse the symbol:

EquationInequality
\(-\frac{x}{3}=4\)\(-\frac{x}{3}>4\)
\(x=-12\)\(x<-12\)

The equation has one solution, −12. The inequality has all numbers less than −12 as solutions; −12 itself is excluded.

Checking one value does not check a whole solution set — Main 6

For \(-15q\le135\), divide both sides by −15 and reverse the symbol to get \(q\ge-9\). Serena is correct: adding 15 does not undo multiplication by −15.

Joaquin’s value \(q=150\) does satisfy the original inequality, but that does not prove that his proposed answer \(q\le150\) is correct. For example, −10 satisfies his answer, yet \(-15(-10)=150\) is not less than or equal to 135. One working value confirms only that value; one counterexample disproves a proposed solution set.

Keep every variable term — Main 7

Serena’s last step loses a variable term. Here are the correct steps:

InequalityReason
\(3x+3\le-2x+5\)Original inequality
\(3x\le-2x+2\)Subtract 3 from both sides.
\(5x\le2\)Add 2x to both sides.
\(x\le\frac25\)Divide both sides by 5; keep the symbol because 5 is positive.

Worked example 2 — Distribute, collect terms, and solve

Original problem — Main 8: Solve \(-4(3m-1)\ge2(m+3)\), explaining the steps.

Worked solution:

InequalityReason
\(-4(3m-1)\ge2(m+3)\)Original inequality
\(-12m+4\ge2m+6\)Distribute on both sides.
\(-14m+4\ge6\)Subtract 2m from both sides.
\(-14m\ge2\)Subtract 4 from both sides.
\(m\le-\frac17\)Divide both sides by −14 and reverse the symbol.

Note: Distributing −4 within the left expression does not reverse the symbol. Dividing both sides by −14 does.

All real numbers or no solutions — Main 9

Sometimes the variable terms cancel. If the remaining statement is true, every real value works. If it is false, none work.

PartSimplificationSolution and number-line graph
(a)\(4s+6\ge6+4s\) becomes \(6\ge6\)All real numbers. Shade the entire number line.
(b)\(3r+5>3r-2\) becomes \(5>-2\)All real numbers. Shade the entire number line.
(c)\(4(n+1)<4n-3\) becomes \(4< -3\)No solutions. Leave the number line unshaded.

Worked example 3 — A coefficient whose sign is unknown

Original problem — Main 10: Solve \(ax+b>c\) for x.

Worked solution: Subtract b from both sides to get \(ax>c-b\). Serena is right: the sign of a determines what happens when you divide.

ConditionSolution
\(a>0\)\(x>\frac{c-b}{a}\) — divide by a positive number; keep the symbol.
\(a<0\)\(x<\frac{c-b}{a}\) — divide by a negative number; reverse the symbol.
\(a=0\)The original inequality becomes \(b>c\). All real x work if \(b>c\); there are no solutions if \(b\le c\).

When \(a=0\), check \(b>c\) directly; you cannot divide by zero.

2. Ready — Amusement-park costs and test scores

In your MVP packetSection 4.6 · Ready · Problems 1–2 · p. 27

These are applications of familiar equations and inequalities. Include the membership fee in total cost, and remember that visits and correct answers are whole-number counts. Try each problem before opening the key.

Check your work — Ready 1–2 answer key

1 — Amusement-park visits

Let v be the number of visits. Each answer below gives the maximum number of visits within the budget.

  1. \(50+15v\le100\), so \(v\le\frac{10}{3}\). 3 visits

  2. \(25v\le100\), so \(v\le4\). 4 visits

  3. \(50+15v\le160\), so \(v\le\frac{22}{3}\). 7 visits

  4. \(25v\le160\), so \(v\le\frac{32}{5}\). 6 visits

2 — Test scores

Let q be the number of correct answers and S the score in points. Each question is worth \(100\div20=5\) points.

  1. \(S=5q\)

  2. \(5q\ge70\), so \(q\ge14\). 14 or more correct answers

  3. \(5q\ge83\), so \(q\ge16.6\). 17 or more correct answers

  4. Initially 12 questions were correct and 8 were wrong. Half of those 8 become correct, giving \(12+4=16\) correct answers: \(\frac{16}{20}\times100\%=80\%\).
