OperationWhat to do
Add or subtract matricesCombine corresponding entries. The matrices must have the same dimensions. Subtraction means subtracting each second-matrix entry from its matching first-matrix entry.
Multiply by a numberMultiply every entry by that number.
Multiply two matricesUse one row from the first and one column from the second. Multiply matching entries, then add the products. The inner dimensions must match.

Volunteer hours and credits — Main 1–3

The packet gives September volunteer hours for drama and chess. Both matrices have rows for tables, floors, dishes and columns for weekdays, weekends:

\[D=\begin{bmatrix}12&4\\8&3\\6&3\end{bmatrix}\]
\[C=\begin{bmatrix}5&2\\3&2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}\]

1 — Combined hours: Add corresponding entries:

\[D+C=\begin{bmatrix}17&6\\11&5\\8&4\end{bmatrix}\]

2 — Nine months of drama hours: Multiply every September entry by 9:

\[9\begin{bmatrix}12&4\\8&3\\6&3\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}108&36\\72&27\\54&27\end{bmatrix}\]

3 — Chess club’s September credit by chore: Multiply the chess hours by the rate column, which gives $4 per weekday hour and $6 per weekend hour:

\[\begin{bmatrix}5&2\\3&2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}4\\6\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}5(4)+2(6)\\3(4)+2(6)\\2(4)+1(6)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}32\\24\\14\end{bmatrix}\]

For example, tables earn \(5(4)+2(6)=32\) dollars. The product has one column of dollar credits, with the rows still tables, floors, and dishes.

Worked example — Find just one product entry

Original problem — Main 5: Find the boxed entries of the packet’s product. Consider the box in row 1, column 2.

Worked solution: The first matrix’s row 1 is \((-2,3)\); the second matrix’s column 2 contains −3 and 5. Use only those values:

\[(-2)(-3)+3(5)=6+15=21\]

There is no need to calculate the other entries to find this one.

The identity matrix — Main 7

An identity matrix is a square matrix with 1s on its diagonal from top left to bottom right, and 0s elsewhere. For three rows and columns:

\[I=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\]

Multiplying a matrix by a matching identity matrix leaves it unchanged. Each identity row selects the corresponding row of the other matrix: for example, the first row gives \(1\times\text{row 1}+0\times\text{row 2}+0\times\text{row 3}\).

To solve Main 7, first find the blank in the left matrix. Call it q. The product’s third-row, second-column entry is 3, so:

\[0(3)+q(2)+1(3)=3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q=0\]

The left matrix is therefore I. The right matrix and the product must be identical. Fill the remaining blanks by matching their positions:
