Use any known point on a line

IN YOUR MVP PACKETMain task · pp. 28–29 · Problems 2–4
Explain why two students can write different-looking equations for a single line and recognize the role of a point on the line.

In a graph, the slope is the change in output divided by the change in input. When the slope is constant, you can start at any known point on the line and move along it using the same rate of change.

Important: In this assignment, you are not finding the value of x that solves an equation. You are simplifying one side to show that both sides are the same expression for every x.

A reliable method

1. Distribute multiplication across every term inside parentheses. 2. When a negative sign precedes parentheses, change the sign of every term inside. 3. Combine the x-terms and number terms separately. 4. Compare the simplified expression with the other side.

Teacher's Review of the Original MVP Packet

The main task develops a valid and valuable principle: a line can be written using its slope and any of its points, not only the y-intercept. Its multiple representations are mathematically equivalent.
