Revisit the ideas from class

This section explains some of the ideas you explored in class through the guided exercises. Use it alongside the “A Practice Understanding Task” portion of Section 1.10 in your MVP packet.

In your MVP packetMain · Tables 1–4 and A–B · pages 45–46
Try the packet task on your own first

Attempt the task before reading worked solutions. Use them to check your reasoning or help if you are stuck.

Count the multiplications between known terms

Geometric means are missing terms inserted so each term is multiplied by the same ratio. Suppose a known value \(A\) becomes \(B\) after \(k\) steps. Multiplying by \(r\) on every step gives:

\[Ar^k=B,\qquad r^k=\frac BA\quad(A\ne0).\]

Find every real ratio that works, then multiply to fill the missing terms. This is different from arithmetic means: divide the endpoint ratio across repeated multiplications, not the total difference across additions.

Worked example — Main Table 1

Original problem: A geometric sequence has term 1 equal to 3 and term 3 equal to 12. Is there only one possible missing term?

Worked solution: There are two multiplications, so \(3r^2=12\), giving \(r^2=4\). Both 2 and −2 work: \(2^2=(-2)^2=4\).

\[3,\ 6,\ 12\qquad\text{or}\qquad3,\ -6,\ 12.\]

Answer: The missing term can be 6 or −6. Do not discard the negative ratio unless the context or directions require positive terms.

Main A–B: explain the method and the possible choices

Count the index gaps, divide the later endpoint by the earlier endpoint, then solve for a repeated multiplier. For these positive endpoints, an even number of gaps allows positive and negative ratios of the same magnitude, because an even number of negative factors produces a positive result. An odd number of gaps gives only the positive real ratio here. Multiply through to verify every proposed sequence.
