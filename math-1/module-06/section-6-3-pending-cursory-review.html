1. In class: A Solidify Understanding Task

Revisit the ideas from class

This section explains some of the ideas you explored in class through the guided exercises. Use it alongside the “A Solidify Understanding Task” portion of Section 6.3 in your MVP packet.

Try the packet task on your own first

Attempt the in-class questions before reading the worked solutions. Then use the explanations below to check your reasoning and work through anything you found difficult.

In your MVP packetSection 6.3 · Main task · Five pre-image/image pairs · pp. 13–14

Give enough information to locate the image

Use the frog’s tongue, toes, and body to track corresponding parts. A slide keeps the same orientation; a turn changes which way the frog faces; a reflection reverses it. Then make your description complete:

MotionInclude in the description
TranslationHow far left/right and up/down.
RotationThe center, the angle, and clockwise or counterclockwise.
ReflectionThe line of reflection, usually given by an equation.
CombinationEach motion in the order you apply it.

Worked example 1 — Frog 1 to frog 2: describe the slide

Original problem: Describe a transformation carrying image 1 to image 2.

Worked solution: The tongue moves from \((5,21)\) to \((11,28)\): 6 right and 7 up. The corresponding toe moves from \((1,20)\) to \((7,27)\), the same change. Translate the whole frog 6 units right and 7 units up.

Worked example 2 — Frog 1 to frog 5: combine two motions

Original problem: Describe a transformation carrying image 1 to image 5.

Worked solution: First reflect across \(x=17\), then translate 12 units down. The tongue \((5,21)\) reflects to \((29,21)\), because 5 and 29 are equally far from 17. Moving down 12 gives \((29,9)\), the tongue of frog 5.

The toe \((1,20)\) reflects to \((33,20)\), then moves to \((33,8)\), matching frog 5. Applying several transformations in order is called a composition of transformations.

Sample descriptions for all five pairs

These use the tongue and toe grid readings above. Use other corresponding features to check the whole image.

3. Set — Reflect points and find mirror lines

In your MVP packetSection 6.3 · Set · Problems 3–10 · pp. 16–17

The mirror line is a perpendicular bisector

A perpendicular bisector crosses a segment at its midpoint and at a right angle. For a point and its reflected image, the mirror line is that perpendicular bisector.

The midpoint is halfway between the endpoints. Find its x-coordinate by averaging the two x-coordinates, and its y-coordinate by averaging the two y-coordinates.

Worked example 3 — Set 3: find the reflected point

Original problem: Reflect A across line m and label its image A′.

Worked solution: Read \(A=(-3,3)\) and \(m:\ y=3x+2\). The perpendicular path has slope \(-\frac13\). A line of that slope through A is \(y=-\frac13x+2\).

Both lines pass through \(M=(0,2)\), so M is where the path meets the mirror. From A to M, move 3 right and 1 down. Repeat that change to find \(A'=(3,1)\).

Worked example 4 — Set 7: find the mirror line

Original problem: Draw the line of reflection carrying P onto P′, and find its equation.

Worked solution: Read \(P=(1,0)\) and \(P'=(5,2)\). Their midpoint is \((3,1)\). The segment PP′ has slope \(\frac{2-0}{5-1}=\frac12\), so the mirror line has slope −2.

Write it as \(y=-2x+b\). It passes through \((3,1)\), so \(1=-2(3)+b\), giving \(b=7\). The line of reflection is \(y=-2x+7\).

Try your constructions before opening the key. Use equal distances on opposite sides of the mirror line for 3–6, and a perpendicular line through the midpoint for 7–10.
