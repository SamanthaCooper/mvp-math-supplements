What makes two lines perpendicular?

Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle (90°). A horizontal line and a vertical line are perpendicular. For two slanted lines, the slopes are negative reciprocals: flip the fraction and change its sign.

Slope of one lineSlope of a perpendicular line
\(2\)\(-\frac12\)
\(\frac13\)\(-3\)
\(\frac25\)\(-\frac52\)
\(-\frac32\)\(\frac23\)

Equivalently, their slopes multiply to −1. The horizontal/vertical case is separate: a horizontal line has slope 0, and a vertical line’s slope is undefined.

Worked example 1 — Experiment 1: rotate the slope triangle

Original problem — steps 1–5: Start with \(A=(2,3)\) and \(B=(4,7)\). Make a right triangle with AB as its hypotenuse, then rotate it 90° about A and compare the hypotenuse slopes.

Worked solution: From A to B, move 2 right and 4 up, so the slope is \(\frac42=2\). Choose \(C=(4,3)\); AC is horizontal and CB is vertical, giving a right angle at C.

Turn the triangle 90° counterclockwise about A. The displacement 2 right and 4 up becomes 4 left and 2 up. Thus \(B'=(-2,5)\), and \(C'=(2,5)\). The new hypotenuse has slope \(\frac{2}{-4}=-\frac12\).

The quarter-turn makes AB and AB′ perpendicular. It also preserves the triangle’s lengths and angles.

Why the rule works

Suppose a segment has horizontal change r and vertical change s, so its slope is \(\frac{s}{r}\). A counterclockwise quarter-turn changes those displacements to \((-s,r)\). Its new slope is \(\frac{r}{-s}\), the negative reciprocal of the original slope. This explains the pattern in all four experiments.

2. Ready — Find distances on a coordinate grid

In your MVP packetSection 6.2 · Ready · Problems 1–4 · p. 10

Horizontal and vertical lengths come from differences in coordinates. For a slanted side, make a right triangle using those changes as the two legs, then use the Pythagorean theorem.

Worked example 2 — Ready 1: measure the slanted side

Original problem: Find the side lengths of the first triangle in Ready.

Worked solution: Its slanted side joins \((-4,-3)\) and \((0,4)\). The horizontal change has length 4 and the vertical change has length 7:

\[d^2=4^2+7^2=65\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d=\sqrt{65}\approx8.06\]

The other two sides have lengths 4 and 7.

3. Set — Draw parallel and perpendicular lines

In your MVP packetSection 6.2 · Set · Problems 5–10 · p. 11

Parallel lines are distinct lines in the same plane that never meet. Slanted parallel lines have the same slope. In \(y=mx+b\), use the same m and a different b to make a parallel line. To make a perpendicular line, use the negative reciprocal slope; you may choose any y-intercept here.

Worked example 3 — Set 8: choose a perpendicular line

Original problem: Graph a line perpendicular to the given line, and give both equations.

Worked solution: The given line is \(y=\frac13x+1\). Its perpendicular slope is −3. One possible new line is \(y=-3x\). Plot \((-1,3)\) and \((1,-3)\), then draw the line through them. These points are far enough apart to make the line easy to draw accurately.

Try each graph before checking the key.
