In your MVP packetSection 6.1 · Main task · Lazy, Lunging, and Leaping Lizard · pp. 1–3

Three ways to move a figure

A transformation moves each point of a figure to a new location. The original figure is the pre-image; the result is the image. A point and its new location are corresponding points. We often label them N and N′ (read “N prime”).

A translation slides every point the same distance in the same direction. A rotation turns every point through the same angle about a fixed center. A reflection flips the figure across a fixed line, like a mirror.

These are rigid motions: they preserve lengths and angles, so the figure keeps its size and shape. The pre-image and image are congruent.

Worked example 1 — Lazy Lizard: slide every point

Original problem: Translate the lizard so its nose moves from \((12,12)\) to \((24,20)\).

Worked solution: The nose moves \(24-12=12\) units right and \(20-12=8\) units up. Every anchor point gets that same change:

\[(x,y)\longmapsto(x+12,y+8)\]

For example, the tail moves from \((19,14)\) to \((31,22)\). Its distance and direction from the nose stay the same.

Worked example 2 — Lunging Lizard: turn about A

Original problem: Rotate the lizard 90° about \(A=(12,7)\) so it dives toward the mud.

Worked solution: Use a counterclockwise quarter-turn. The nose starts 5 units above A, so it ends 5 units left of A, at \((7,7)\).

To move another point, first describe its position relative to A. A counterclockwise quarter-turn changes a horizontal/vertical displacement \((u,v)\) into \((-v,u)\). Then measure that new displacement from A.

The tail \((19,14)\) is 7 right and 7 up from A. After the turn it is 7 left and 7 up, at \((5,14)\). Each point stays the same distance from A; the two segments from A to a point and its image meet at 90°.

Worked example 3 — Leaping Lizard: reflect across a slanted line

Original problem: Reflect the lizard across \(y=\frac12x+16\).

Worked solution: Draw a perpendicular path from a point to the mirror line. Continue the same distance on the other side. The mirror line meets the segment joining a point and its image at a right angle, halfway between them.

For the nose \(N=(12,12)\), that halfway point is \(M=(8,20)\). From N to M, move 4 left and 8 up. Repeat that change from M to get \(N'=(4,28)\). Use a ruler and a right-angle tool to make the same construction for the other anchors.

Grid note — Leaping Lizard, pp. 2–3

Teacher’s review of the source packet

Main reflection, pp. 2–3: The grid stops at y = 32, but three reflected anchors have larger y-coordinates. A grid extending to y = 36 gives enough room.

Teacher edition, Purpose for 6.1 (PDF p. 6): The perpendicular segments in a 90° rotation are the segments from the center to a point and its image. Segments joining various pre-image/image pairs are not generally perpendicular to one another. The student explanation above uses the center-to-point segments.
