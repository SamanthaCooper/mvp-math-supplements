In your MVP packetSection 5.2 · Main task · Problems 1–8 and final recommendation · pp. 6–7

Two quantities, one constraint

Let \(x\) be the number of cats and \(y\) the number of dogs. A cat costs $32 and a dog costs $80, so the $1,280 budget gives

\[32x+80y\le 1280\]

A linear inequality in two variables describes a constraint involving two quantities. A solution is an ordered pair that makes the inequality true. Here, \((10,12)\) means 10 cats and 12 dogs: their cost is \(32(10)+80(12)=1280\), so they fit the budget.

The father’s suggestion of 12 cats and 12 dogs costs $1,344. It is $64 over budget.

Main 4–7 — The boundary line and its region

The combinations costing exactly $1,280 lie on the boundary line, \(32x+80y=1280\). Solving for \(y\) gives

\[y=-\frac{2}{5}x+16\]

The slope is \(-\frac{2}{5}\): trading two dogs for five cats keeps the cost the same. Points above this line cost more than the budget; points below it cost less. Shade the region below the line, and include the line because spending exactly $1,280 is allowed.

Main 7: \(32x+80y\le1280\). Equivalently, \(y\le-\frac25x+16\).

Main 8 — The space constraint

Each cat needs 6 square feet and each dog needs 24 square feet. The available 360 square feet gives

\[6x+24y\le360\]

The boundary is \(y=-\frac14x+15\). Again, shade below the line and include the boundary.

For this situation, use nonnegative whole-number coordinates: the shaded regions help us see the constraints, but pet counts must be whole numbers.

A recommendation

Ten cats and twelve dogs use \(6(10)+24(12)=348\) square feet, cost $1,280, and bring in $320 per day if all spaces are occupied. This satisfies both constraints. Section 5.1 explains why $320 is the largest possible daily income under this budget; other combinations can also achieve it.

Worked example — Set 9

For \(y>-\frac35x+2\), draw the boundary through \((-10,8)\) and \((10,-4)\). Make it dashed because equality is excluded. The point \((0,3)\) satisfies the inequality: \(3>2\). The point \((0,0)\) does not: \(0>2\) is false. Shade above the line.
