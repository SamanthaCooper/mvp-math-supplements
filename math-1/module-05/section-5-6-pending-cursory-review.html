A quick graphing routine

Rewrite each inequality to isolate \(y\), graph its boundary, and shade its side. Keep only the overlap: a solution must make every inequality true. \(<\) and \(>\) exclude the boundary, so use a dashed line; \(\le\) and \(\ge\) include it, so use a solid line. Multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses an inequality sign.

Main 1 — Between two boundaries

Original problem:

\[\begin{cases}-5x+3y\le45\\2x+3y>24.\end{cases}\]

Worked solution: Add or subtract the x-term, then divide by 3:

\[y\le\frac53x+15.\]\[y>-\frac23x+8.\]

Draw a solid upper boundary through \((0,15)\) and \((-9,0)\). Draw a dashed lower boundary through \((0,8)\) and \((12,0)\). Shade above the lower line and on or below the upper line.

The two lines meet at \((-3,10)\), which is not included because it lies on the strict boundary. For example, \((0,10)\) is a solution: \(30\le45\) and \(30>24\).

Main 2 — Equivalent first constraint, different second constraint

Original problem:

\[\begin{cases}-10x+6y\le90\\6x+9y>36.\end{cases}\]

Worked solution: Divide the first inequality by 2 and the second by 3. Positive division preserves each sign:

\[\begin{cases}-5x+3y\le45\\2x+3y>12.\end{cases}\]

Thus \(y\le\frac53x+15\) and \(y>-\frac23x+4\). The first boundary is the same as in 1; the second has y-intercept 4 instead of 8. Shade above the dashed line and on or below the solid line.

Do not assume the two systems are equivalent just because some coefficients are multiples. The second inequality of 2 simplifies to \(2x+3y>12\), not \(2x+3y>24\). For example, \((0,6)\) satisfies system 2 but fails system 1.

Main 3 — Check the boundary point

Original problem: Is \((-3,10)\) a solution to system 1? Why or why not?

Answer: No. It gives \(-5(-3)+3(10)=45\), satisfying \(45\le45\), but \(2(-3)+3(10)=24\), which fails \(24>24\). Equality does not satisfy a strict inequality.

Main 4 — Read the three sides of a triangle

Original problem: Write a system for the shaded triangle in graph 4.

Worked solution: Read the corner points \((-6,1)\), \((0,10)\), and \((3,\frac52)\). The three solid boundaries are \(y=\frac32x+10\), \(y=-\frac52x+10\), and \(y=\frac16x+2\). For example, the lower side rises \(\frac32\) over a run of 9, giving slope \(\frac16\). Keep points below the two steep lines and above the shallow line:

\[\begin{cases}y\le\frac32x+10\\y\le-\frac52x+10\\y\ge\frac16x+2.\end{cases}\]

Check an interior point such as \((0,5)\): \(5\le10\), \(5\le10\), and \(5\ge2\) are all true.

Main 5 — Reversing all three signs gives no common region

Answer: Amanda's picture is not the solution to a system with all three signs reversed. That system would require

\[\begin{cases}y\ge\frac32x+10\\y\ge-\frac52x+10\\y\le\frac16x+2.\end{cases}\]

No point is above both steep lines and below the shallow line. The first and third conditions would require \(x\le-6\), while the second and third would require \(x\ge3\). A single x-value cannot meet both. The solution set is empty, meaning it contains no points.

The separate shaded wedges in Amanda's picture satisfy only some of the reversed conditions. A system requires all three at once.

Main 6 — Define the amounts before writing the food model

Let \(T\) be scoops of Tabitha Tidbits and \(F\) scoops of Figaro Flakes. Fractions of a scoop are allowed, and \(T\ge0\), \(F\ge0\). The nutrient totals are \(4T+12F\) grams of protein and \(6T+4F\) grams of fat.

Packet wording issue — Main 6–7, printed p. 24

The stated targets are “at least 8 grams of protein and no more than 6 grams of fat per scoop.” A total-meal interpretation uses those same numbers as limits for the entire meal. The teacher notes do not explicitly resolve this wording mismatch. These produce different solution regions for batches larger or smaller than one scoop. The two readings below are labeled separately.

Reading the printed targets literally: nutrients per scoop

A nonempty mixture contains \(T+F\) scoops. Divide each nutrient total by that amount:

\[\frac{4T+12F}{T+F}\ge8.\]\[\frac{6T+4F}{T+F}\le6.\]

Because \(T+F>0\), multiply both sides by it without changing the inequality direction. The protein condition becomes \(4T+12F\ge8T+8F\), or \(F\ge T\). The fat condition becomes \(6T+4F\le6T+6F\), which holds whenever \(F\ge0\).

Per-scoop solution

Use nonnegative amounts with \(F\ge T\) and \(T+F>0\). At least half of the mixture must be Figaro Flakes. All such mixtures meet the fat limit; the empty mixture is excluded.

Alternative classroom reading: totals for one meal

If the targets are repaired to mean at least 8 grams of protein and at most 6 grams of fat in the entire meal, the model is

\[\begin{cases}4T+12F\ge8\\6T+4F\le6\\T\ge0,\\F\ge0.\end{cases}\]

For the graph, isolate \(F\): shade on or above \(F=-\frac13T+\frac23\) and on or below \(F=-\frac32T+\frac32\), keeping nonnegative coordinates. Both nutrient boundaries are solid.

The corners are \((0,\frac23)\), \((0,\frac32)\), and \((\frac57,\frac37)\). They locate the boundaries; estimate them from the graph and check by substitution. This bounded triangle differs from the per-scoop region, where increasing a batch without changing its proportions preserves the nutrient amounts per scoop.

Main 7 — Three recipes and the proposed mixture

Sample recipes: Each row makes exactly one scoop, so its nutrient totals also equal its nutrients per scoop. Each uses both foods and satisfies both interpretations.

Tabitha scoopsFigaro scoopsProtein (g)Fat (g)
\(\frac12\)\(\frac12\)85
\(\frac14\)\(\frac34\)10\(\frac92\)
\(\frac18\)\(\frac78\)11\(\frac{17}{4}\)

Proposed mixture: One scoop of Tabitha plus half a scoop of Figaro contains \(4(1)+12(\frac12)=10\) grams of protein and \(6(1)+4(\frac12)=8\) grams of fat in \(\frac32\) scoops.

Under the total-meal interpretation it fails the fat maximum, since \(8>6\). Under the printed per-scoop interpretation it fails the protein minimum: \(10\div\frac32=\frac{20}{3}<8\) grams per scoop. Its fat is \(8\div\frac32=\frac{16}{3}\le6\) grams per scoop. Thus it is not acceptable under either reading, for different reasons.

3. Set — Parallel boundaries can leave a strip or no overlap

In your MVP packet

Section 5.6 · Set · Problems 10–15 · p. 25

These two boundary lines have the same slope \(\frac34\) and different y-intercepts, so they are parallel. At every x-value, \(\frac34x-2\) is 5 units below \(\frac34x+3\). Whether a system has solutions depends on which side of each line is allowed.

Attempt both graphs and the comparison before opening the key.

Check your work — Set 10–15

10 — Shade between the lines

The solution is the open strip

\[\frac34x-2<y<\frac34x+3.\]

Both lines are dashed because both inequalities are strict.

11: The lines are parallel: both have slope \(\frac34\).

12 — Sample answers: \((0,0)\), \((0,1)\), and \((4,3)\). At \(x=0\) the bounds are \(-2<y<3\), so 0 and 1 work. At \(x=4\) the bounds are \(1<y<6\), so 3 works.

13–14 — No point can satisfy both

The reversed system requires \(y<\frac34x-2\) and \(y>\frac34x+3\). It demands a value below the lower line and above the higher line simultaneously. There are no solutions, so three solution points cannot be named.

15: Both graphs have the same two dashed parallel boundary lines. Problem 10 has a common strip between them; problem 13 has no common region. Reversing the signs changes the sides allowed, not the lines themselves.

Teacher's review of the source packet

Main 6–7, printed p. 24: “Per scoop” requires nutrient totals to be divided by total scoops. The alternative model \(4T+12F\ge8\) and \(6T+4F\le6\) constrains total nutrients in a meal; it is a possible classroom interpretation, not the literal per-scoop reading. Remove “per scoop” from the target sentence to match that model, or retain the printed wording and use \(F\ge T\) for nonempty nonnegative mixtures. The supplement labels both readings and supplies recipes valid under both.

Teacher notes, PDF p. 48: The variables are described as ounces, but student data are given per scoop. Use scoops unless an ounces-per-scoop conversion is supplied.

Main 5 is an intentional reasoning question: Amanda's claim is false, as the teacher notes explain. It does not require a packet-error warning.
